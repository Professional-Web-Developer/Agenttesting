# Level 3: final decision

**Winner: Sirattai Kaasu (சிரட்டை காசு, "money for coconut shells").**

We collect coconut shells from eateries every week, weigh them in front of the owner, and **pay cash on the spot**. We then sell the shells in lots to shell-charcoal and activated-carbon units, which are short of raw material.

## Why it won, when 11 others lost
1. **It puts cash in someone's hand.** The eatery gets paid for something it treats as rubbish today. It isn't a number people could look up themselves (F7).
2. **The payer is certain.** A charcoal unit buying raw material it is short of pays per kg on delivery. That's a trade, not a referral fee.
3. **No gatekeepers.** The eatery owner decides alone. There's no official, committee, licence or creditor tie, **as long as temples are excluded** (HR&CE tenders).
4. **It works on cash and paper.** A hanging scale and a paper receipt are enough, and WhatsApp is optional.
5. **The timing is right.**
   - Shell prices rose sharply over 2025–26: Kerala ₹5–9/kg → ₹28–35/kg; Pollachi ₹29,000–34,500/tonne in 2026.
   - Activated-carbon exporters are publicly complaining about the shortage.
   - Coconut yields in the Pollachi belt are down, so the shortage should last at least into December (market auditor).

## The two patterns that killed the rest
- **"Bill photo → number → seller pays a referral fee."** Eight candidates had this shape. The founder's F7 objection is correct: information people can get themselves, paid for by sellers who can bypass you, is not a business.
- **Creditor ties.** Whoever lends the person money (the agri dealer, milk centre, pawnbroker or waste merchant) controls where they buy or sell.

## Decisions and conditions for Level 4
| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Pilot in one town 60–200 km from the Pollachi–Kangeyam–Udumalpet buyer belt, but outside the coconut-growing belt itself.** Examples: Karur, Dindigul, Madurai, Trichy, Erode town, Salem. The founder picks the nearest | Inside the belt, informal buyers already collect. Too far away, and freight eats the spread |
| **Suppliers:** tiffin centres, messes, hotels, sweet shops and chutney-heavy eateries. **Exclude** temples (tendered shell rights) and tender-coconut vendors (soft husk, not hard shell) | Keeps F3 clean and the grade right |
| **Don't bundle Gas Kanakku at launch** | Red team: it dilutes focus, and its revenue model failed F7 |
| **Sell lots of 1–1.5 tonnes for cash on delivery, with no storage yard at first** | Keeps the float under ₹35k and avoids a municipal trade licence |
| **Software is the operating system:** rate engine, weigh-in receipts, route sheets, lot profit ledger, validation tracker | The founder's advantage over informal traders is transparency (a published fair rate, a receipt for every weigh-in) and the ability to run many routes and collectors on one system |

## Honest fit note for the founder
**No pure-software idea survived your seven tests.** Every software-only candidate was either information people can get free (F7) or paid by sellers who can bypass you. Sirattai Kaasu is a trading business run on software. You can start it without handling shells yourself by hiring a goods auto and one collector per route. But you, or someone you trust, must attend the first two pickup rounds and the first buyer sale.

## Kill criteria (from the 2-week test; decided in advance)
**Keep going only if all of these hold:**
- ≥ 60% of surveyed eateries get nothing or ≤ ₹10/kg today;
- ≥ 25 eateries sign up;
- ≥ 1 tonne is collected in 2 weeks;
- net spread ≥ ₹3/kg after transport;
- ≥ 1 buyer commits to weekly purchases.

**Stop if any of these happens:**
- most eateries already get ≥ ₹20/kg;
- fewer than 10 sign up;
- the net spread is under ₹1.5/kg;
- the buyer's delivered price falls below about ₹25/kg while eateries still expect ₹18 or more. That is break-even at the base-case assumptions (red team §3).

## Key risks
1. **A commodity cycle.** If coconut yields recover, shell prices could fall back towards ₹10/kg. *Response:* the rate engine recalculates the eatery rate from the buyer price every week, so we pay less when we're paid less and are never locked in.
2. **Informal buyers may already pay eateries.** *Response:* the week-1 survey measures this before any money is committed.
3. **Kitchen staff may treat shells as their perk.** *Response:* share part of the payment with staff, or pay the staff member who fills the sacks.
4. **Moisture and kernel make eatery shells wet.** *Response:* grade at pickup and use a clear moisture deduction on the receipt.
