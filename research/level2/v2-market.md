## 2. Priority facts: what I verified

1. **Coconut shells.**
   - **Price.** No fresh Pollachi, Kangeyam or Kerala *shell* price for Jul–Sep 2026 was found **[unverified]**. The latest hard data:
     - Palakkad shops paid up to ₹28/kg and resold to TN wholesalers at ₹30/kg in Jan 2026 ([Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2026/01/09/rising-value-coconut-shells.html)).
     - Pollachi hit ₹29,000/t in May 2026 and ₹34,500/t later in 2026 (L1, Dinamalar, not re-checked).
     - **Supporting the price:** Pollachi nut yields are down 30–60% ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/news/tamilnadu/farmers-see-hope-to-regrow-wilting-coconut-trees)) [attr?]. Activated-carbon import prices averaged US$2,502/t in 2025, against $2,078/t in 2024 ([ICC Dec 2025](https://coconutcommunity.org/page-content/market-review-of-coconut-shell-charcoal-december-2025-mr-88)). India exported 98,347 t of activated carbon in Jan–Jul 2025 ([ICC Aug 2025](https://iccweb.coconutcommunity.org/page-content/market-review-of-coconut-shell-charcoal-august-2025-mr-84)).
     - **Against it:** Kerala traders said availability "has now improved enough to meet current demand" (Jan 2026).
   - **Who buys.** Shell-charcoal and activated-carbon units. Kangeyam taluk has shell-burning units, some of them operating illegally [attr?] (same search). No named Pollachi, Coimbatore or Tiruppur unit list was found beyond the IndiaMART listings in L1.
   - **What eateries, temples and vendors do today.**
     - **Eateries in non-coconut TN cities: still [unverified].** This is the single most important unknown.
     - **Temples:** shell rights are typically *tendered* (AP example: [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/andhra-pradesh/confrontation-over-coconut-shell-tender-at-kolanubharathi-temple-1987671)). Treat temples as gatekeepers under F3 and exclude them.
     - **Tender-coconut vendors** produce soft green husk, not hard shell, so they are not a charcoal feedstock [A].
   - **Organised aggregators.** None found for TN eateries. The informal trade is efficient: the **₹2/kg trader margin** in Palakkad is the benchmark the founder's spread will be squeezed towards.

## 3. Evidence notes by candidate (F4 and F6 reasoning)

- **Sirattai Kaasu.**
  - **F4 is 3, not 4.** A fast-growing informal trade already exists: boards outside Kerala shops, door-to-door collectors in Karnataka and AP, and Kerala-to-TN wholesale flows. It runs on about ₹2/kg margins.
  - The organised, fixed-rate weekly pickup for TN city eateries is real white space, but informal buyers will follow any visible volume.
  - **F6 is 5.** The customers sit on the same streets (tiffin centres, messes, sweet shops), one collector route is itself the marketing channel, and pickup is a weekly repeat. The message is plain cash ("₹__/kg at your door").
  - **Market fact that matters most:** the eatery's current price. If city eateries already get ₹15+/kg from informal buyers, the spread is gone.
- **Gas Kanakku.**
  - F4 gap is real. Only hardware sellers, generic tips and a state subsidy compete.
  - F6 is 4: the same eateries as Sirattai (zero extra acquisition cost if bundled), but trust in a "savings share" is slow to build.
  - The market headwind is that LPG prices fell by about ₹366 net over Jul–Sep.
- **Marundhu.**
  - F4 gap is real: every agri app found earns from selling inputs.
  - F6 is 3: farms are scattered, but shandies and agro-shop queues are natural contact points, and there are 2–3 seasons a year.
  - Best timing of the info-style ideas (samba season).
- **Paal Vilai.**
  - F4 is real and F6 is 4: farmers gather at collection centres twice a day, and slips come every 10–15 days.
  - The flush season (Oct–Mar) weakens the dairy-pays model in the launch window.
- **Vandi Vilai.**
  - Best timing and largest volume. F4 is partial.
  - Structurally the same as Sedharam (information plus a voluntary dealer referral). On-road calculators are everywhere, so the founder's "people can check it on sites" objection applies directly.
- **Kazhivu Rate.** The market facts point against a gap:
  - many listed merchants;
  - an exporter-level platform (Reverse Resources);
  - open-end mills complaining that waste is *too expensive*.
- **Adagu.** The switch is done by well-funded lenders who market directly, and the neutral register adds little that they can't copy.
- **Service Check and Veedu Check.** Crowded by aggregators and AI contract tools (L1 evidence stands).
- **Velinaadu and Virundhu.** Partial gaps. F6 is limited by a one-off need (Velinaadu) and a short buying window (Virundhu).

## 4. My ranked top 3 (market and competition view)

1. **Sirattai Kaasu.**
   - It is the only candidate where the customer gets **cash in hand** and the payer is **a buyer taking delivery**, not a referral. That makes F7 structurally strong.
   - Its audience is the most concentrated and cheapest to reach: street by street, with a weekly repeat.
   - The shell price is supported through Dec 2026 by the Pollachi yield collapse and high global carbon prices.
   - **Conditions:**
     - operate in TN towns *outside* the Kongu coconut belt (e.g. Madurai, Trichy, Chennai suburbs), where no collector was found;
     - plan for a **₹2–4/kg** spread, not ₹3–6, because Kerala traders survive on ₹2;
     - verify in week 1 what eateries get today.
2. **Gas Kanakku, as a module on the Sirattai route ("Kitchen Kanakku").**
   - It has a real F4 gap and the same concentrated customers, so the weekly shell visit is the free marketing channel.
   - It's a module, not a standalone, because LPG prices are easing and the TN induction subsidy offers eateries another way out.
   - Standalone urgency is weaker than in May.
3. **Marundhu Bill Check.**
   - Of the remaining ideas it has the cleanest F4 gap: no neutral same-molecule checker exists, and every incumbent sells inputs.
   - It has the best Oct–Dec timing: samba season, fertiliser shortage and overpricing.
   - It repeats every season.
   - **Warning for the red team:** it is information-led. It passes F7 only if the flat dealer listing fee (₹299–499/month, not per referral) proves a sure payer.

**Runners-up:**
- **Vandi Vilai:** the best timing and market size, but it is Sedharam-shaped, and BikeFair is unresolved.
- **Paal Vilai:** a real gap, but the flush-season payer is weak.

**Dropped on F4:** Service Check, Veedu Check, Kazhivu Rate and Adagu Check (each scored 2).
