# Scout C: Rural and informal India (Tamil Nadu focus)

Level 1 research scout report. Date: 2026-09-28.
Domain: farmers, dairy and livestock owners, fishers, daily-wage, construction and migrant workers, and women's SHGs.
Method: I brainstormed 29 raw problems and killed every one that failed a filter. For the survivors I ran more than 40 WebSearch queries in total, at least 2 per survivor. I picked the top 3 and parked one runner-up.

Conventions: **[A]** marks an assumption that needs validation. **[unverified]** marks a figure that appeared in a search summary but that I could not check against the primary source. WebFetch was not used, per the brief, so all facts come from search-result summaries.

## 2. Candidate #1: Marundhu Bill Check (agri-input bill check)

**One-line pitch:** A farmer's family member sends a WhatsApp photo of the agro-shop bill or the pesticide bottle, or the farmer reads it out on a phone call. They get back a Tamil voice note: "This is molecule X. The same molecule at the same strength costs ₹___ under another brand. These two items on your bill are the same chemical. This add-on was forced on you. This product is not on the approved list." It also lists fair-price shops nearby.

### The problem (with evidence)
- **Many brands of the same molecule.** The Insecticides Act registration process produced about 2.5 lakh pesticide products from only about 280 registered molecules ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/875775/lethal-dose-indian-farmers-are-dying-because-the-government-is-regulating-pesticides-poorly)). Pesticides have no price control ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/farmers-feel-the-heat-of-soaring-pesticide-prices-111072800052_1.html)).
- **The price gap for an identical active ingredient is 3–4x.** One example is chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC, 150 ml:
  - FMC Coragen costs ₹2,250 on [Agrowala](https://www.agrowala.com/product/insecticides/coragen-150-ml) and ₹2,495 (MRP ₹2,710) on [Agroshopy](https://agroshopy.com/index.php?route=product%2Fproduct&product_id=185).
  - Sudarshan's chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC costs ₹569 on [KisanShop](https://www.kisanshop.in/product/sudarshan-chlorantraniliprole-185-sc-insecticide).
  - Katyayani Chakraveer costs about ₹650 for 150 ml ([Katyayani](https://katyayanikrishidirect.com/products/katyayani-chakraveer-chlorantraniliprole-18-5-sc-insecticide)) [unverified pack size].
  - The patent expired in August 2022, and many generics followed ([AgriBusiness Global](https://www.agribusinessglobal.com/markets/22-active-ingredient-patents-to-expire-by-2030-what-it-means-for-indian-agrochemical-companies/)).
  - Caveat: these are online prices. Village shop prices are [unverified], and co-formulant quality can differ.
- **The dealer is the advisor, and the dealer has sales targets.** Private dealers filled the gap left by government extension services. Only about 13% of farmers follow extension-officer tips ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/875774/indian-farmers-spray-a-toxic-cocktail-of-pesticides-because-the-government-lacks-staff-to-guide-them)). One study found that retailer recommendations raise the probability of overuse by about 58–62% [unverified attribution; it appeared in search summaries of the [Springer 2026 paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10460-026-10852-2) and related work].
- **Forced tagging with fertiliser.**
  - Dealers bundle biostimulants, nano-urea and similar products with subsidised urea and DAP. This adds ₹200–400 per bag ([Tribune, Karnal](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/farmers-cry-foul-over-traders-forcing-add-ons-with-fertilisers)).
  - The Union minister wrote to the states in July 2025 to stop it ([InsightsIAS](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2025/07/21/biostimulants/)), and the FCO was amended in March 2026 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/magazine/deep-dive/story/shattered-dreams-how-indias-nano-fertiliser-strategy-came-a-cropper-525605-2026-04-14)). Complaints continue.
- **Products that are not even approved.** Around 30,000 biostimulant products were in circulation. After provisional registrations ended in June 2025, only 146 are approved ([Krishi Jagran](https://krishijagran.com/news/over-8-000-biostimulant-products-lose-registration-only-146-cleared-under-fco/), [Global Agriculture](https://www.global-agriculture.com/india-region/india-ends-provisional-registration-of-biostimulants-only-146-products-now-approved-under-fco-9352-products-cancelled/)).
- **Tamil Nadu specifically:** about 50 fertiliser traders had licences suspended or cancelled in the 2025–26 kharif season for duplicate or substandard fertiliser, hoarding and similar malpractice ([The420](https://the420.in/tn-50-fertiliser-traders-licences-cancelled-kharif-malpractices/)).

### Solution mechanism (a Tamil Nadu village, step by step)
Example: Murugan, a 2-acre chilli farmer near Paramakudi (Ramanathapuram). He has a basic phone, and his son in Madurai uses WhatsApp.
1. At the agro shop the dealer writes a slip: Coragen 150 ml, "growth tonic" 500 ml, 1 bottle of nano-urea "with" 2 bags of DAP. Total ₹5,200. Murugan pays cash or takes it on credit.
2. **Channel A:** his son sends a photo of the slip, or of the bottles, to the Marundhu Check WhatsApp number. **Channel B:** Murugan gives a missed call to a printed number. A helper calls back within the day, and Murugan reads the product names aloud. **Channel C:** a local helper (for example a student or tea-shop owner who is given a sticker and QR code) sends the photo for him.
3. Within minutes a **Tamil voice note** comes back, along with a simple image card:
   - "Coragen = chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC. Brand X and Brand Y have the same chemical at the same strength for about ₹570–650."
   - "The growth tonic is not on the approved biostimulant list."
   - "Nano-urea is not compulsory with DAP. Tagging is illegal."
   - "Your ₹5,200 bill could have been about ₹2,900." [illustrative]
4. The card also names 1–3 **fair-price shops** within about 10 km that stock those generics at posted prices, each with a short code. Murugan can also show the card to his own dealer and ask for the generic.
5. The shop confirms the code on WhatsApp when he buys. This is how referrals are tracked, since purchases are in cash.
6. Ten days later a callback or WhatsApp asks, "Did it work?" The answers build village-level ratings of generic brands, which answers the "cheap means weak" fear.

The tool **never diagnoses pests**, which limits liability and avoids competing with Plantix. It only makes factual claims: same molecule, same concentration and formulation, label dose per acre, whether the product is registered or approved, and whether items are duplicates on the bill.

### Who saves money, and roughly how much
- The farmer saves. Illustration [A]: a 2-acre chilli or cotton farmer buys several premium-brand bottles a season. If 2 of them have same-molecule generics that are 50–75% cheaper, that saves about ₹2,000–4,000. Avoided tag-alongs at ₹200–400 per bag across 2–4 bags save about ₹400–1,600. **The total is roughly ₹2,500–5,500 per farmer per season [A, validate in week 1].**
- Frequency: 2–3 seasons a year and many sprays per season. That is far more frequent than a jewellery purchase.

### Who pays you, and why
1. **Fair-price agro dealers (primary).** They get new footfall from farmers the tool redirects. Tracking commission on cash sales is hard, so a flat **₹299–499 per month listing** is more realistic than a percentage [A]. Dealer margins are reported at about 5–15% ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/how-industries-work/agriculture-and-food/how-the-agricultural-chemicals-industry-works/)), so they cannot pay much, and a flat fee is kinder to them.
2. **Indian generic formulators (secondary).** Mid-size brands need rural distribution and already spend on dealer schemes. They could sponsor "availability" (which shops stock them) but **never rank**, which is always price per acre-dose.
3. **Later: anonymised retail-price data** for agrochemical companies and researchers.

Realism: at ₹399 per month × 20 dealers per district this is modest (about ₹8k per month per district [A]). It becomes a real business only with density across several districts, or with the vet-medicine and cattle-feed extension. The farmer always pays nothing.

### Competitors found, and why the gap still exists
- **Plantix**: disease diagnosis that [pivoted to a pesticide marketplace taking commission](https://www.ehn.org/plantix-shifts-from-pesticide-reduction-to-agri-supply-chain-dominance).
- **AgroStar, DeHaat, BharatAgri (Krushi Dukan), BigHaat**: advisory combined with input e-commerce ([Inc42 list](https://inc42.com/startups/27-agritech-startups-disrupting-agricultural-landscape-in-india/)).
- **FarmerChat (Digital Green)** ([Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.digitalgreen.farmer.chat)), **Seedhi Baat by iKonTel** ([link](https://www.ikontel.com/post/fast-crop-diagnosis-through-whatsapp)) and **Kisan AI**: crop-problem chatbots, not bill or price checkers.
- **Farmer.in pesticide guide** ([link](https://www.farmer.in/pesticides/)): a static web guide to doses and prices, not bill-driven and not in Tamil voice.
- **Uzhavan (TN government app)**: schemes, prices and weather.
- **Why the gap exists:** every funded player earns from **selling** inputs, so none has an incentive to tell a farmer "you are overpaying for the same chemical" or "you don't need this". It is the agri-input version of the generic-medicine gap, but pharmacies got Truemeds and agro shops got no one.

### Biggest reason it could fail
**Trust and credit ties.** Many farmers buy on credit from one dealer and trust his word more than a bot. They also fear that cheaper generics are weak or fake; spurious products are a real share of the market. If farmers don't act on the report, dealers won't pay.

### 2-week validation test
- Place: one chilli/cotton or vegetable belt, for example the Paramakudi/Mudukulathur chilli belt, the Oddanchatram/Theni vegetable belt, or Perambalur cotton.
- **Week 1:** collect 60 real input bills through relatives' WhatsApp, 2 shandy visits and 1 village tea shop. Produce the savings report by hand (founder plus a spreadsheet). Visit 10 agro shops and record the shelf prices of the generics.
- **Week 2:** send the reports as Tamil voice notes, then make follow-up calls on day 10–14.
- **Keep going if:**
  - the median identified saving is ≥ ₹500 per bill, or ≥ 20% of the bill;
  - ≥ 30% of farmers say they bought, or will buy, the cheaper equivalent at the next purchase (at least 10 verified by the shop code);
  - ≥ 2 dealers agree to pay ₹299+ for a listing.
- **Stop if** the median saving is < 10%, or < 15% of farmers act.

### MVP (1 developer, 2–4 weeks, under ₹50k)
- WhatsApp Business Cloud API bot (Meta pricing to verify; user-initiated service replies are cheap or free [unverified]) plus a missed-call number (Exotel/Knowlarity class, a few thousand rupees a month [unverified]).
- Image to text: an LLM vision API reads the bill or label (brand, molecule, concentration, pack size, price). A human-in-the-loop admin screen lets the founder correct the reading before replying for the first 200 bills.
- Product database:
  - the molecule–brand–price table is seeded from online stores (BigHaat, KisanShop, Katyayani, AgroStar) and local shelf prices;
  - registration status comes from the public CIB&RC registration lists and the FCO-approved biostimulant list.
- Reply: a Tamil TTS voice note plus a generated image card showing old bill, new bill and savings.
- Referral codes and a dealer dashboard (a simple web page). Farmer follow-up is automated.
- Phase 2: vet medicines and mineral mixture for dairy owners, using the same engine.

## 3. Candidate #2: Paal Vilai Check (milk-slip check)

**One-line pitch:** A photo of the milk payment card or slip, or the litres, fat and SNF read out on a call, gets back a Tamil voice note: "Your amount is correct or short by ₹__. For your fat and SNF this fortnight: Centre A pays ₹__, Centre B ₹__, the Aavin society ₹__. Your fat reading dropped while your neighbours' didn't, so ask for a retest."

### The problem (with evidence)
- **Rates are volatile and the gaps are large right now.**
  - In July 2026, Aavin procured at about ₹34/L while private dairies offered ₹45–50/L. Farmers selling directly to households got ₹60–62 ([News Today, 21 Jul 2026](https://newstodaynet.com/2026/07/21/private-milk-prices-surge-aavin-faces-supply-strain/)).
  - Aavin then raised the procurement price to ₹41 and then ₹44/L at 4.3% fat and 8.2% SNF from 1 Sep 2026 ([The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/states/south/tamil-nadu/milk-procurement-price-hiked-rs-3-per-litre-aavin-cm-vijay-254023)).
  - Private dairies have raised their rates again since ([SocialNews, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.socialnews.xyz/2026/09/25/tamil-nadu-milk-agents-seek-aavin-price-review-as-private-dairies-raise-rates/); [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/tamilnadu/milk-shortage-looms-in-tamil-nadu-as-private-dairies-set-to-hike-prices-by-rs-4litre)).
- **Farmer anger.** In August 2026 a statewide agitation took place and milk was poured on highways ([Dairy Industry Expo](https://west.dairyindustryexpo.com/tamil-nadu-dairy-farmers-protest-for-higher-milk-prices-and-loan-waiver/)).
- **Payment depends on a fat/SNF rate chart the farmer cannot see or check** ([Dudh Hisaab explainer](https://dudhhisaab.com/blog/how-milk-collection-centers-work)). SNF-reading manipulation has been alleged against a private dairy in Chittoor, Andhra Pradesh ([Sakshi Post](https://www.sakshipost.com/news/andhrapradesh/chittoor-dairy-farmers-cheated-heritage-foods-name-snf-150736)); that is an allegation, from a neighbouring state.
- **Dairies are short of milk** and competing for suppliers (same sources). This means there is a buyer side willing to pay for farmers.

### Solution mechanism (step by step)
Example: Selvi, 3 cows, a village near Tiruchengode (Namakkal). She pours about 15 L a day at a private collection centre and gets a paper card or 10–15-day slip.
1. Her daughter photographs the slip and sends it on WhatsApp. Or Selvi calls a missed-call number, and a helper calls back to note her litres, fat, SNF, rate and amount.
2. The system rebuilds each dairy's **current rate chart** from crowdsourced slips (many farmers at the same centre) and from Aavin's published benchmark. It then:
   - checks the arithmetic (litres × rate for that fat/SNF);
   - compares options within about 3 km for **her** fat and SNF, including payment frequency and delays;
   - detects anomalies, for example a sudden drop in fat readings at one centre across many farmers while neighbours at other centres stay steady.
3. She receives a Tamil voice note and an image card: "You were paid correctly / ₹___ short. Centre B pays about ₹2 more for your milk, 1.2 km away."
4. Selvi decides alone: switch, or negotiate with her current centre ("B pays more").
5. If she moves to a centre that is a paying partner, the centre confirms after 30 days of pouring.

### Who saves money, and how much
- The dairy household. Illustration [A]: 15 L/day × ₹2/L better rate = ₹30/day, about **₹11,000 a year**. Catching one slip error or a fat under-reading adds more. The size of gaps between private dairies within a village is **unverified**, and it is the key thing to validate.

### Who pays, and why
- **Private dairies and collection-centre operators** who are short of milk (see evidence) pay per new supplier who stays ≥ 30 days, for example **₹200–500 per farmer**, or ₹0.10/L for 6 months [A]. Ranking stays purely by computed rupees per litre, and all centres are listed whether or not they pay.
- The farmer pays nothing.

### F1–F6 check
| Filter | Pass? | Reason | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Pass | Paper milk card or slip, a photo by one family member, or read out on a call | 4 |
| F2 | Pass | Free. The farmer only gains | 5 |
| F3 | Pass (caveat) | The farmer alone chooses where to pour milk. No official, society secretary or committee is needed. Caveat: advances or feed credit from the current centre | 4 |
| F4 | Pass | All tools found serve the **buyer**: [Mobile Dairy Center](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=dairy.mobile.com.mobiledairy&hl=en_GB), [Dhenu](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.quartex.dhenu&hl=en), [DairyKhata](https://dairykhata.in/milk-collection-software-app), [Liter](https://www.liter.live/), MilkMate, Dudh Hisaab. No farmer-side cross-dairy comparison was found | 4 |
| F5 | Partial | Easy to build, but it needs dense crowdsourced slips per centre, and dairies paying for leads is unproven | 3 |
| F6 | Pass | Milk price is a hot topic in Aug–Sep 2026. Dairy farmers' WhatsApp groups, Tamil reels, collection-centre queues | 4 |

### Competitors, and why the gap exists
The competitors are the collection-centre and dairy ERP apps listed above, plus livestock marketplaces (Animall, Pashushala). Dhenu shows a farmer their **own** pours and rate chart, but only inside one dairy's system. **The gap exists because every paying customer in this market is a buyer.** No one makes money telling the seller that the buyer down the road pays more.

### Biggest reason it could fail
**Switching may be physically or socially impossible.** There may be only 1–2 collection points within reach, milk is perishable and poured twice a day, and advances, feed credit and Aavin society ties hold farmers in place. Village word of mouth may already spread the rate. If gaps within a village are under ₹1/L, the product is a curiosity.

### 2-week validation test
- 3 villages in one dairy belt (Namakkal, Erode, Salem or Krishnagiri). Collect 60 slips, map every collection point within 3 km and its rate chart.
- **Keep going if:** ≥ 40% of farmers have an option ≥ ₹1.5/L better within reach, **or** ≥ 10% of slips show errors or under-reading anomalies; ≥ 10 farmers switch or negotiate a better rate; ≥ 1 private-dairy procurement manager or centre operator agrees to pay per new supplier.
- **Stop if** gaps are < ₹1/L and no switching happens.

### MVP (2–4 weeks)
- WhatsApp bot plus missed-call callback, and slip OCR via LLM with human review.
- A rate-chart database per centre (built from slips, updated each cycle). A comparison engine by fat and SNF.
- Anomaly alerts (a z-score on fat readings per centre per cycle). Tamil TTS reply and image card.
- A simple partner dashboard for centres.

## 4. Candidate #3: Velinaadu Velai Check (overseas job and agent check)

**One-line pitch:** Before paying an agent for a Gulf, Malaysia or Singapore job, the worker or family sends the offer letter, visa copy and agent card on WhatsApp, or calls. Within minutes they get a Tamil answer: is the agent registered, is the fee legal, what are the red flags, and what did returnees from our area actually pay.

### The problem (with evidence)
- **Size.** About 2.2 million Tamil Nadu emigrants live abroad, about 1.1 million of them in the GCC ([TN Migration Survey 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317750220_Tamil_Nadu_migration_survey_2015)). TN accounted for about 8% of GCC-bound emigration clearances in 2025 ([Mahad Manpower research](https://www.mahadmanpowers.co.in/research/india-to-gcc-manpower-statistics-2026/); this is a recruiter's blog, a secondary source).
- **Legal cap versus reality.** The legal cap is **₹30,000 + GST**, but workers are overcharged **₹2–5 lakh** by unregistered agents ([Embassy of India, Qatar advisory](https://www.indianembassyqatar.gov.in/whats_new?id=MjM1); [Embassy Algiers](https://www.indianembassyalgiers.gov.in/page/advisory-on-overcharging-by-agents-for-overseas-recruitment-offering-fake-overseas-jobs-and-illegal-recruitment/)).
- **Recent Tamil Nadu cases:**
  - July 2026, Coimbatore: more than 500 job seekers cheated of about ₹7 crore ([ANI](https://aninews.in/news/national/general-news/tamil-nadu-two-arrested-for-allegedly-cheating-over-500-job-seekers-of-83777-crore-with-false-overseas-employment-promises20260717215830/)).
  - A Bengaluru agency took at least ₹1.5 lakh each from about 300 TN workers ([The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/states/south/tamil-nadu/how-a-fake-job-agency-in-bengaluru-cheated-300-unskilled-workers-in-tn-182377)).
  - The TN government has issued a youth advisory ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Ftamil-nadu%2Ftamil-nadu-govt-issues-advisory-to-youth-against-fake-overseas-job-offers-3039491)).
- **The data exists but is not used.** eMigrate lists more than 1,800 active recruiting agents and **3,094 illegal agents** ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!international/emigrate-portal-ensuring-safety-and-security-of-indian-blue-collar-workers-enn24101405549)). Victims still don't check it.

### Solution mechanism (step by step)
Example: Karthik, 24, from Mudukulathur. A village "sub-agent" asks for ₹1.8 lakh for a "Saudi driver visa".
1. Karthik, or his mother through a relative's WhatsApp, sends photos of the offer letter, visa copy and agent's card, and says the amount. Or he calls the missed-call number.
2. The system:
   - matches the agent name or registration number against the eMigrate **active RA** and **illegal agent** lists (public data, refreshed manually);
   - computes the legal maximum (₹35,400 including GST) and the excess demanded;
   - reads the offer for red flags: visit visa for work, no employer licence details, payment to a personal account, WhatsApp-only contact, no receipt;
   - shows **what TN returnees reported paying** for the same country and job, plus peer reviews of that agent. This is a Tamil version of the ITUC Recruitment Advisor model.
3. Karthik gets a Tamil voice note: "This agent is not registered. Legally no one can take more than ₹35,400. 4 red flags. Here are currently open registered-agent/OMCL openings for drivers." The tool never recruits or collects money.
4. Karthik decides alone.
5. After he lands, optional help with cheaper remittance (the revenue hook).

### Who saves money, and how much
- The worker's family. Avoided fraud is **₹1.5–5 lakh** per incident (sources above). Avoided overcharge is roughly ₹50k–1.5 lakh [A]. The value per event is high, but events are rare per family (once per migration).

### Who pays, and why (the weak point)
1. **An optional "thank-you" after the visa is stamped**, ₹500–999, paid out of the lakhs saved. This is F2-compliant but voluntary and unpredictable [A].
2. **Remittance-partner referrals** once the worker is abroad. For example, Remitly's partner programme pays **up to about $20 per new customer, depending on corridor** ([Remitly partner programme](https://www.remitly.com/us/en/landing/partner-program); [Affpaying](https://www.affpaying.com/remitlyaffiliateprogram)). The UAE/Saudi-to-India payout is **unverified**.
3. **Do not** take per-placement fees from recruiting agents. That would make the service an unlicensed sub-agent under the Emigration Act, and the licence requirement would break F3.

Ability to pay: low and uncertain. This is the reason it ranks third.

### F1–F6 check
| Filter | Pass? | Reason | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Pass | Migrating youth usually have WhatsApp. Families use one phone or a voice call | 4 |
| F2 | Pass | Free. Only an optional payment from savings | 4 |
| F3 | Pass (caveat) | The worker alone decides. Uses only public lists. Must never act as a recruiter, which would need a licence | 4 |
| F4 | Partial | Government services and the ITUC Recruitment Advisor cover parts of it (see below) | 3 |
| F5 | Weak | The MVP is trivial, but the revenue model is unproven | 2 |
| F6 | Strong | Fraud stories spread fast. Gulf-migrant villages (Ramanathapuram, Pudukkottai, Sivaganga, Thanjavur) have dense WhatsApp groups | 5 |

### Competitors, and why the gap exists
- **eMigrate** (MEA): RA list, illegal-agent list, and a 24x7 multilingual helpline since v2.0 in Oct 2024 ([Travelobiz](https://travelobiz.com/india-launches-new-e-migrate-portal-to-simplifies-overseas-job-process-for-workers/)).
- **TN Commissionerate for Non-Resident Tamils**: helpline and pre-departure orientation ([nrtamils.tn.gov.in](https://nrtamils.tn.gov.in/en/)).
- **OMCL**: TN government recruiting agency ([omcmanpower.tn.gov.in](https://omcmanpower.tn.gov.in/)).
- **ITUC Recruitment Advisor**: peer reviews of agencies. Launch languages were English, Indonesian, Nepali and Tagalog ([ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/new-online-ratings-help-migrant-workers-avoid-unscrupulous-recruiters)).
- **NextMigrate**: guides ([link](https://nextmigrate.com/blog/fake-overseas-job-offers-how-to-verify)).
- Blue-collar job portals: WorkIndia, Vahan, MyJobee.
- **Gap:** none is Tamil, voice- or WhatsApp-first, reads the actual offer document, **or** gives fee benchmarks from local returnees. Government portals are English/Hindi web forms that workers don't open before paying.

### Biggest reason it could fail
Revenue. A secondary risk is that workers trust the relative or sub-agent more than a bot, and there is liability if the tool calls something "safe" that later proves fraudulent. It must say "no red flags found", never "genuine".

### 2-week validation test
- Post 3 Tamil reels ("₹1.8 lakh கேட்டாங்களா? சட்டப்படி ₹35,400 தான்"), meaning "Did they ask you for ₹1.8 lakh? The legal limit is only ₹35,400". Seed the reels in 5–10 Gulf-migrant village WhatsApp groups and 2 passport/photo shops.
- **Keep going if** there are ≥ 50 check requests in 14 days, ≥ 20% carry a serious red flag, and ≥ 10 people say they would pay ₹500 after success, or ≥ 5 opt in to a remittance referral.
- **Stop if** there are < 20 requests.

### MVP (2 weeks)
- WhatsApp bot, with the eMigrate RA and illegal-agent lists downloaded and indexed. LLM red-flag reading of offer documents. A fee calculator.
- A returnee survey form (voice or WhatsApp) to build fee benchmarks and agent reviews. Human review of every answer during the pilot.

## 5. Runner-up (parked): Patti Check (net-price check on commission-agent sale slips)
- **Idea:** photograph the commission agent's sale slip (patti) and get back the net ₹/kg after commission, hamali (loading charges), "kazhivu" (deductions) and advance interest. Compare with other agents' same-day nets.
- **Evidence:**
  - At Oddanchatram about 70% of farmers sell through commission agents, who charge 10% commission ([TNAU/Krishikosh study](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/dab37a62-0f56-411c-80b7-7d480ca8dcc9/content)).
  - TN regulated markets report undue deductions and trader associations that stifle competition ([study](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/2bed/6b312bb7a01144f0f0f778aa4a49d0842ebe.pdf)).
  - There are suspicions of tampered scales in TN ([The Hindu via PressReader, Feb 2026](https://www.pressreader.com/india/the-hindu-tiruchirapalli-9wwf/20260221/281612426868313)).
- **Why parked:**
  - It needs many same-day slips per market to compare fairly, a cold-start problem.
  - Quality differences confuse the comparison.
  - Agents already earn 10% and have no reason to pay.
  - Farmers are often tied by advances.
- **Scores:** F5 = 2, F6 = 3.

## 6. Notes for Level 2
- **#1 Marundhu Bill Check could share a brand and engine with Sedharam Check** ("___ Check": bill photo → one comparable number → fair-price sellers). They target different people, so they don't compete, and one WhatsApp infrastructure serves both.
- Across all three ideas, the recurring failure mode in rural TN is **tied credit**: the dealer, milk-centre operator or sub-agent who lends money gets to decide. Validation must measure actual switching, not stated interest.
- Numbers that still need primary verification: village shop prices of generics compared with online prices; within-village gaps between private dairies; Remitly's Gulf-to-India payout; the number of licensed agro dealers in TN (not found).
