# Scout D: Young people in Tamil Nadu, and new problems from 2024–2026

Level 1 scout report · 28 Sep 2026 · Domain: (a) students, job seekers, freshers, salaried people in their 20s–30s, people who've just moved to Chennai, Coimbatore or Bengaluru; (b) new problems created by changes in 2024–2026.

Everything marked **(assumption)** or **(unverified)** needs checking. Every other number has a source link.

## 0. Headline

- I brainstormed **36 raw problems**. **32 were killed, 1 was parked** as out of domain, and 3 survived. Most of the "new" problems in (b) died on the filters:
  - digital-arrest scams, quick-commerce pricing and EV battery repair are already crowded (F4);
  - labour codes, PF and PM-JAY need a government office (F3);
  - telecom tariff hikes have no one to pay (F5);
  - smart meters are on the rejected list.
- Several (b) changes **feed into candidate #1** instead:
  - the GST 2.0 price cut (Sep 2025);
  - IRDAI's Sep 2026 draft that cuts dealers' motor-insurance commission;
  - Tamil Nadu's 0% road tax on EVs (2026–27);
  - the two-helmet rule.
- **The best candidate is #1, Vandi Vilai Check** (வண்டி விலை Check, a check on the price of a new two-wheeler). A young buyer sends a WhatsApp photo of the showroom's quotation slip. The tool separates the **statutory** items (road tax, registration, mandatory insurance), which can be calculated exactly, from the **padding**:
  - handling charges;
  - "compulsory" accessories;
  - marked-up dealer insurance;
  - extended warranty;
  - flat-rate finance.

  It then points the buyer to dealers whose quotes have been clean. It is free for buyers, and partner dealers pay per delivered vehicle.
- It is the **same engine as Sedharam Check**: a paper bill, turned into one honest number, with verified cheaper sellers. The saving per purchase is smaller (about ₹3–10k, assumption) than on a gold sovereign. But the item is standardised (same model, same ex-showroom price), so comparing shops is *easier* than with jewellery designs. **Level 2 should treat it as a possible second vertical, or a fallback, for the Sedharam engine.**
- #2 (Service Check, a second opinion on a two-wheeler service estimate) and #3 (Veedu Check, a rental-terms check before signing) pass the filters but are clearly weaker.

## 3. Candidate #1: Vandi Vilai Check (வண்டி விலை Check)

**Pitch:** Send a WhatsApp photo of the showroom's quotation slip and find out, in Tamil, which rupees are government charges and which are the dealer's padding. You also see which nearby dealers quoted the same bike cleanly.

### The problem (with evidence)

**1. Most of the "on-road price" is not the vehicle, and part of it is padding.**
- A typical on-road breakdown includes **handling charges of ₹1,500–₹4,000** on top of ex-showroom price, RTO, insurance and a ₹500 smart card ([BikeOnRoadIndia](https://bikeonroadindia.com/)).
- Handling, accessory and "logistics" charges are negotiable dealer charges, not government fees ([PolicyBazaar](https://www.policybazaar.com/motor-insurance/two-wheeler-insurance/articles/buying-a-new-bike-how-to-negotiate-for-the-best-price/), [BikeDetailsHub](https://bikedetailshub.com/articles/on-road-price-guide-india/), [priceindia](https://priceindia.in/know-hidden-costs-two-wheeler-purchase/)).
- Riders report inflated "RTO charges" on two-wheelers ([Team-BHP: "Two-wheeler dealers overcharging road tax?"](https://www.team-bhp.com/forum/motorbikes/188788-two-wheeler-dealers-overcharging-road-tax-2.html)).
- Some buyers are told accessories are compulsory ([Team-BHP: "Have to buy dealer-forced accessories"](https://www.team-bhp.com/forum/modifications-accessories/253496-have-buy-dealer-forced-accessories-ones-should-i-pick.html)).

**2. It happens in Tamil Nadu, and it's in Tamil news.**
- Dinamalar (Coimbatore) reported a consumer-commission order to compensate a buyer overcharged on a bike ([Dinamalar](https://www.dinamalar.com/news/tamil-nadu-district-news-coimbatore/bike-ordered-to-be-compensated-for-overcharging/4325955)).
- Tamil Oneindia ran "Bike showrooms cheating customers by increasing the price" ([Oneindia Tamil](https://tamil.oneindia.com/news/tamilnadu/bike-showrooms-cheating-customers-increasing-the-price-278415.html)).
- Tamil DriveSpark reports a transport authority ordering dealers to display price charts ([DriveSpark Tamil](https://tamil.drivespark.com/off-beat/automobile-dealerships-must-display-price-chart-transport-authority-030781.html)). Which state issued the order is *unverified*.
- In one reported case, a showroom added ₹2,166 to an electric bike bill and refunded it when the buyer questioned the receipt (from the Tamil search summary; *treat as anecdotal*).

**3. Dealer insurance is a big hidden margin.**
- Motor-insurance commissions average **24% (range 13–50%)**. Dealers and OEM-linked brokers earned **₹7,050 crore in commission on ₹29,000 crore of premium in FY25**. Premiums rose ~34% from FY23 to FY25 while commissions rose ~259% ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/insurance/buying-a-new-car-your-dealer-may-earn-up-to-38-commission-on-motor-insurance-irdai-moves-to-rein-it-in/article-200834/)).
- One buyer was quoted ₹53,000 for dealer insurance vs ₹25,000 outside, for a car ([The Swipe Up, Aug 2026](https://www.theswipeup.com/2026/08/no-car-or-showroom-insurance-what.html)).
- The advice to "never buy insurance from a dealer" appears in two-wheeler guides too ([BankBazaar](https://www.bankbazaar.com/two-wheeler-insurance/guide/say-no-to-your-dealers-two-wheeler-insurance.html)).

**4. New changes (2025–26) make the quote even harder to read. This is the (b) angle.**
- **GST 2.0 (22 Sep 2025):** two-wheelers up to 350cc moved from 28% to 18% GST, cutting ex-showroom prices by ₹5,000–₹24,500 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/auto/2w-prices-may-drop-by-up-to-24-500-as-automakers-pass-on-gst-cut-gain-125091201094_1.html)).
- **TN EV road tax:** 100% road-tax exemption for EVs registered 1 Jan 2026 – 31 Dec 2027 (per [Acko](https://www.acko.com/road-tax/tamilnadu-road-tax/) / [GoDigit](https://www.godigit.com/road-tax/tamilnadu-road-tax) summaries; *verify against the TN Government Order*). So any "road tax" line on an EV quote is a red flag.
- **Helmets:** a rule requires two BIS helmets to be supplied with every new two-wheeler ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/growth/govt-proposes-to-make-two-helmets-mandatory-for-new-2-wheelers), [BikeAdvice](https://bikeadvice.in/two-helmets-mandatory-on-purchase-of-new-two-wheelers-from-jan-2026/)). *Final status unverified.* This could become a new excuse for "compulsory accessories".
- **ABS:** the ABS mandate may be deferred ([DriveSpark](https://www.drivespark.com/two-wheelers/2025/govt-may-delay-abs-mandate-for-sub-125cc-two-wheelers-079059.html)).
- **IRDAI draft, Sep 2026:** proposes **nil commission on third-party cover and 5% on own-damage cover for new-vehicle insurance** ([Business Standard, 24 Sep 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/insurance/irdai-s-draft-distribution-norms-could-put-insurance-volumes-at-risk-126092401304_1.html), [RetailIntel: could squeeze dealer-led insurance from FY28](https://retailintel.in/signal/irdai-s-proposed-commission-caps-could-squeeze-dealer-led-in-cec35860)). *Hypothesis:* if dealers lose insurance income, they'll push handling and accessories harder, which makes an independent check more valuable.

**5. Why dealers pad.**
- Two-wheeler dealer margins are thin, about **3–6% of ex-showroom**, and FADA is asking OEMs for 7% ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news-national/fada-asks-indian-oems-to-revise-margins-to-a-minimum-7-56503), [91wheels](https://www.91wheels.com/news/car-dealership-margins-explained)).
- Add-ons make up the difference. Service is said to be 18% of dealer revenue but 40–55% of profit ([FRANticc](https://franticc.com/compare-2w); *third-party estimate, unverified*).

**6. Market size.**
- India registered **2.02 crore two-wheelers in CY2025** ([Autocar India](https://www.autocarindia.com/bike-news/two-wheeler-sales-cross-20-million-mark-in-438709)).
- Tamil Nadu was **8.0% of India's two-wheeler sales in Oct–Dec 2025** ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/uttar-pradesh-tops-indias-two-wheeler-sales-in-q3-fy2025-26-131388)).
- That gives **≈16 lakh two-wheelers a year in TN** *(assumption: 8% applied to the full year)*.
- First vehicles are bought mostly by young people: students, freshers, gig riders and newly married couples.

### Solution mechanism: how a real person in Tamil Nadu uses it

Example: Karthik, 23, just joined a factory job in Sriperumbudur. He visits a Honda dealer in Tambaram for an Activa and gets a handwritten or printed quotation slip.

1. **He sees a Tamil reel** ("Showroom quotation-la ₹6,000 extra-va? Photo anuppunga"), or a friend forwards the WhatsApp number. He, or his father on the family's one WhatsApp phone, sends a **photo of the slip**. Optionally he adds a voice note ("Activa, Tambaram, cash-la vaanguren").
2. **The bot reads the slip** (OCR plus an LLM) and turns it into line items: ex-showroom, road tax, registration/number plate/smart card, insurance, handling, accessories, extended warranty, "other", and finance details if it's a loan.
3. **It checks each line:**
   - **Ex-showroom:** compared with the brand's published TN price for that variant.
   - **Road tax:** recalculated exactly (10% if ex-showroom is up to ₹1 lakh, 12% above, per [BankBazaar](https://www.bankbazaar.com/tax/tamil-nadu-road-tax.html) and [Acko](https://www.acko.com/road-tax/tamilnadu-road-tax/); 0% for EVs in 2026–27, *verify*).
   - **Registration and smart-card fees:** compared with a fee table.
   - **Insurance:** compared with outside quotes for the same model from 2–3 insurers, collected manually at first.
   - **Handling, accessories and extended warranty:** marked "optional / negotiable".
   - **Finance:** a flat rate is converted to its true APR, and processing fees and bundled "EMI protection" are flagged.
   - **Exchange offer:** if there is one, the old bike's value is compared with a used-price reference.
4. **The reply comes in Tamil**, as a short voice note plus a text card:
   - "Government charges: ₹X (correct). Dealer's extras: ₹Y. Of those, ₹Z are optional. Ask for these to be removed."
   - A **ready-to-show message** he can show the salesman.
   - "Last month, 2 dealers near you quoted this model cleanly: Dealer A ₹…, Dealer B ₹…"
5. **If he buys from a partner dealer**, he shows the referral code the bot generated. The dealer confirms delivery on WhatsApp, and **the dealer pays us**.
6. **If he negotiates at his original dealer instead**, he still saves money. We keep his (anonymised) quote in our dealer database, which gives us more evidence of who pads and who doesn't.
7. **After delivery**, the bot reminds him of free-service due dates. This leads naturally into candidate #2.

Payment method doesn't matter (cash, loan or UPI). The paper slip is the input, and only one person in the family needs WhatsApp.

### Who saves money, and roughly how much

An illustrative scooter in Chennai *(all figures are assumptions, to be replaced with data from the 2-week test)*:

| Line | Fair / statutory | Typical padded quote |
|---|---|---|
| Ex-showroom | ₹90,000 | ₹90,000 |
| Road tax (10%) | ₹9,000 | ₹9,000 |
| Registration, HSRP plate, smart card | ~₹1,200 | ₹2,500 ("RTO charges") |
| Insurance (1-yr own-damage + 5-yr third-party) | ~₹6,500 (outside quote) | ₹7,500 |
| Handling | ₹0 | ₹2,500 |
| "Compulsory" accessories | ₹0–800 (helmets, if genuinely required) | ₹3,000 |
| Extended warranty | optional | ₹1,500 |
| **Avoidable** | | **≈ ₹8,000–₹9,000** |

- **Buyer:** saves roughly **₹3,000–₹10,000 per vehicle** *(assumption)*. For a fresher on ₹15–25k a month, that's 2–4 weeks' pay. Dealer finance on a flat rate can add several thousand more *(assumption)*.
- **Scale:** if 1% of TN buyers use it, that's 16,000 people × ~₹5,000 ≈ ₹8 crore a year saved *(assumption)*.

### Who pays you, and why

- **Main payer: partner dealers and sub-dealers with a "clean on-road" pledge.** They pay **₹300–₹500 per delivered vehicle** *(assumption; test in validation)*. Why they would:
  1. Margins are thin (3–6%), so **volume** matters. Paying only per delivered sale is cheaper than walk-ins that don't convert.
  2. A new buyer becomes a **service customer for years**, and service is the dealer's high-margin line (FRANticc estimate above).
  3. If IRDAI caps insurance commission, clean dealers will need volume even more.
- **Later:**
  - cars (bigger tickets and bigger referral fees);
  - partner garages via #2.
- **Not in the MVP:** insurance referrals. Paying for insurance leads needs an IRDAI-licensed intermediary, which conflicts with F3's "no licences". Any insurance tie-up must go through an already-licensed partner and be checked first.
- **Rough revenue:** 20,000 checks in year 1 (Chennai + Coimbatore), 15% buying through partner dealers, at ₹400 each ≈ **₹12 lakh a year** *(assumption)*. That's modest. The upside is in cars and service.

### Competitors found, and why the gap still exists

**Why the gap exists:** incumbents make money *from dealers* (leads, ads), so auditing dealers is against their interest. The quotation slip is paper and local, and the ex-showroom price is the same everywhere, so price-comparison sites assumed there was nothing to compare. The variance is entirely in the extras.

### Biggest reason it could fail

**Nobody pays.** In a given area there may be only 1–3 authorised dealers for a brand, and they may all pad the same way. With thin margins, few may agree to pay per sale. Buyers may simply use the free check to negotiate at their original dealer: great for them, zero revenue for us.

Mitigations:
- charge only on delivery;
- sell the service relationship, not just the sale;
- include sub-dealers in towns;
- extend to cars (MyCarHelpline shows car buyers engage);
- fall back to an optional "tip from what you saved".

Second risk: purchases are rare (one every 5–8 years), so we need constant new users. Reels are cheap, so this is manageable.

### 2-week validation test (29 Sep – 12 Oct 2026, before Ayudha Pooja)

1. **Is the spread real? (supply side).** Mystery-shop **12 showrooms**: Chennai (Tambaram, Porur, Ambattur) and Coimbatore; 3 top-selling models (for example a 110cc scooter, a 125cc commuter bike, one EV); 2 dealers per brand per city. Ask for itemised written quotes.
   - **Keep going if:** same-model non-statutory extras differ by **≥ ₹2,500** between dealers in the same city, **or** ≥ 50% of quotes contain ≥ ₹3,000 of optional or incorrect items.
2. **Will young buyers send slips? (demand side).**
   - Launch a manual "concierge" WhatsApp number (the founder answers using a spreadsheet).
   - Post 5 Tamil reels and posts in 10 WhatsApp groups (college alumni, IT-park new joiners, delivery-rider groups), plus r/Chennai.
   - **Keep going if:** **≥ 40 real slips from strangers** in 14 days; ≥ 60% say the reply was useful; **≥ 10 report getting ≥ ₹1,000 removed**.
3. **Will anyone pay? (payer side).** Pitch 15 dealers and sub-dealers.
   - **Keep going if:** **≥ 3 agree in writing** (a WhatsApp message is fine) to pay ≥ ₹300 per delivered sale for a 1-month pilot.
- **Stop if:** fewer than 15 slips, **or** the median avoidable amount is under ₹1,500, **or** zero dealers agree to pay.

### MVP (1 developer, 3–4 weeks, under ₹30k *(assumption)*)

- **Week 1:**
  - WhatsApp Business Platform number (Meta Cloud API or a provider such as Gupshup/Interakt; *check current pricing*) and a webhook backend (Node or Python);
  - photo → JSON line items using an OCR/vision LLM;
  - Tamil and English reply templates;
  - text-to-speech for voice notes.
- **Week 2: the rules engine:**
  - TN road-tax formula, with an EV exemption flag;
  - fee table (registration, HSRP plate, smart card);
  - **ex-showroom price table for the top ~40 models** in Chennai/Coimbatore (entered by hand from brand websites);
  - insurance benchmark table (manual quotes);
  - flat-rate → APR calculator;
  - optional/negotiable flags.
- **Week 3:**
  - quote database (Supabase/Postgres) of dealer × model × date × line items;
  - "clean dealer" list;
  - referral codes and dealer delivery confirmation over WhatsApp;
  - a small admin page.
- **Week 4 (optional):** a weekly "cleanest quotes this week" card that also feeds the reels, and free-service reminders (the bridge to #2).
- **Reuse:** the OCR → honest number → verified seller pipeline is shared with Sedharam Check. Building one makes the other cheaper.

## 4. Candidate #2: Service Check (two-wheeler service estimate, second opinion)

**Pitch:** Before you approve the service-centre estimate, send us a photo. We tell you in Tamil which items are due for your km, which aren't, and what a fair price is.

### The problem (with evidence)
- Authorised centres are described as adding **₹2,000–5,000 in parts "found" during service**, marking up oil by **30–60%**, and replacing air filters at 3,000 km when the manual says 12,000 km ([Ride N Repair](https://www.ridenrepair.com/blog-details/bike-service-cost-in-india-2026-complete-price-guide); *note: this is a service seller's blog, so it may be biased*).
- A basic general service costs **₹800–1,500 at authorised centres vs ₹450–1,000 at doorstep providers** ([Ride N Repair checklist](https://www.ridenrepair.com/blog-details/bike-general-service-checklist-india-2026-what-to-expect)). See also [CarBike360](https://www.carbike360.com/articles/bike-service-cost-guide-india-2026-how-much-to-pay-at-every-service-interval).
- A Coimbatore consumer commission ordered **₹35,000 compensation** for deficient bike repair ([Dinamalar](https://www.dinamalar.com/news/tamil-nadu-district-news-coimbatore/order-to-pay-rs-35000-in-compensation-for-service-deficiency-in-bike-repair/4143802)). Refund and re-repair rights are explained [here](https://righttoinformation.wiki/bike-service-refund-re-repair-rights-india).
- Service is the dealer's profit engine: 18% of revenue, 40–55% of profit ([FRANticc](https://franticc.com/compare-2w), *unverified*).
- **Gig riders** do 100+ km a day. They spend **₹1,000–1,500 a month on maintenance** plus ₹150–300 a day on fuel ([GigHour](https://www.gighour.com/blog/delivery-job-salary-india)), so they service often and feel padding most.

### Solution mechanism (step by step)
1. Selvi, 27, a Swiggy rider in Coimbatore, gets a job-card estimate for her scooter. She sends a photo plus her odometer reading.
2. The bot reads the line items and checks them against the **manufacturer's maintenance schedule** for her model and km. It flags items that are "not due", "check before approving" or "OK".
3. Parts are compared with a genuine-parts price table (built from brand price lists where published, otherwise from bills we collect). Labour is compared with local rates we collect.
4. She gets a Tamil voice note and a card: "Approve items 1–4. Ask why item 5 is needed (air filter due at 12,000 km, you're at 7,800). Items 6–7 are optional."
5. If her bike is out of warranty, the bot lists 2–3 **partner garages with a fixed rate card**. They pay us per job.
6. While a bike is in warranty, the bot only advises what to decline at the authorised centre. It never suggests something that could void the warranty.

### Who saves money, and how much
- About **₹500–2,000 per visit** where padding is found *(assumption)*, 3 visits a year for commuters and monthly for gig riders. Roughly **₹1,500–6,000 per rider per year** *(assumption)*.

### Who pays you, and why
- **Partner independent garages** pay ₹50–100 per job, or ₹500–1,000 a month for a listing *(assumption)*. They win customers away from authorised centres and need steady volume.
- **Bundled with #1:** dealers who paid for the sale want the service customer, so they may pay to be listed as "fair-estimate" centres.

### F1–F6 check
| Filter | Pass? | Reason | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | ✅ | Paper job cards, cash payments, one WhatsApp user | 5 |
| F2 | ✅ | Free for riders; garages pay. Small payments per job | 4 |
| F3 | ✅ | The rider alone approves or declines. Warranty makes riders cautious, so during warranty we only advise, never redirect | 4 |
| F4 | ⚠️ Pass, weak | Service aggregators with price lists exist: [BikeFixup](https://bikefixup.com/browse/presence/chennai) (Chennai + 29 cities), [GoBumpr](https://www.amazon.com/GoBumpr-Car-Service-Bike/dp/B01BTRBPRE) (Chennai, since 2015), [Apna Mechanic](https://www.apnamechanic.com/bike-service-app/), [Ride N Repair](https://www.ridenrepair.com/), [BikeWale service locator](https://www.bikewale.com/service-centers/), and for EVs [My EV Services](https://myevservice.in/about) and [ev.care](https://ev.care/service/cities/chennai). None neutrally audits *another* garage's estimate, but once we refer to garages we start to look like them | 3 |
| F5 | ⚠️ Pass | Reuses the #1 engine (+1 week for maintenance-schedule tables). Revenue per job is tiny | 3 |
| F6 | ✅ | Tamil reels ("Service centre bill-la ₹2,000 extra?"), delivery-rider hubs, petrol-bunk posters | 4 |

### Competitors and the gap
The players are listed under F4. **The gap:** each of them *sells* a service, so none can neutrally judge a rival's estimate. A second opinion based on the manufacturer's schedule, in Tamil on WhatsApp, doesn't exist as far as I found. But the gap is narrow: any aggregator could add an "upload your estimate" feature.

### Biggest reason it could fail
Much padding is **defensible** ("brake shoes at 60% wear, better to change"), so we can't always say "not needed". Real, uncontested savings may average only a few hundred rupees, which isn't enough for riders to bother or for garages to pay.

### 2-week validation test
- Collect **50 service estimates or bills** (via reels, 3 delivery-rider hubs in Chennai and Coimbatore, 2 college groups), manual concierge.
- **Keep going if:** ≥ 40% contain ≥ ₹500 of flaggable items; ≥ 15 riders report declining at least one item; **≥ 5 garages** agree to a ₹50/job or ₹500/month pilot.
- **Stop if:** fewer than 20 bills, or fewer than 20% have flaggable items.

### MVP (2–3 weeks, on top of #1)
- Maintenance-schedule table for the top 20 models (from owners' manuals).
- Parts-price table.
- Labour-rate benchmarks from collected bills.
- Garage directory with a signed rate-card pledge.
- Referral codes.
- Same WhatsApp bot and database as #1.

**Recommendation:** don't run #2 as a separate company. Make it **the retention loop of #1**: the buyer gets free-service reminders, then estimate checks, and the partner dealer or garage pays.

## 5. Candidate #3: Veedu Check (வீடு Check, rental terms check before you sign)

**Pitch:** New to Chennai or Coimbatore? Before you pay the advance, send us the terms. We give you the house's *true* monthly cost, the day-1 cash you'll need, and the clauses that will cost you later, with a Tamil or English script to negotiate.

### The problem (with evidence)
**Advance (deposit):**
- In Chennai, owners "settle for 4 to 6 months" advance ([Quora](https://www.quora.com/What-is-the-approximate-advance-to-be-paid-for-renting-a-house-in-Chennai)).
- 10-month advances are spreading from Bengaluru ([KannadaGottilla](https://kannadagottilla.com/10-months-advance-welcome-to-the-new-bengaluru/)).
- **New (b):** reports in Dec 2025 say the "Home Rent Rules 2025" cap residential advance at **2 months' rent**, with online registration within 2 months ([News Today Chennai](https://newstodaynet.com/2025/12/03/new-rules-cap-advance-rent-to-two-months-for-landlords/), [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/real-estate/story/model-tenancy-act-how-new-security-deposits-rent-agreement-entry-exit-rules-will-benefit-tenants-landlords-504839-2025-12-03), [LegalKart](https://www.legalkart.com/en/legal-blog/new-rent-rules-2025-explained-online-registration-&-deposit-limits-made-simple)). *Whether this applies in TN, and how, is unverified*; one source says TN's 2017 Act allows more. We would use it only as a **negotiating point, never as something to enforce**.

**Electricity charged by the landlord:**
- A Chennai hostel charged ₹12/unit where ₹6.35/unit applied ([LawyersClub](https://www.lawyersclubindia.com/experts/charging-of-higher-per-unit-charge-for-electricity-in-hostel-660941.asp)).
- A PG charged ₹11–13/unit on opaque sub-meters ([Sudhir Rao](https://sudhirrao.com/landlord-overcharging-for-electricity-in-rented-accommodation-legal-recourse/)).

**Exit costs:**
- Painting and cleaning deductions at move-out are common ([eVaakil](https://evaakil.com/landlord-deducting-for-paint/), [NoBroker](https://www.nobroker.in/blog/security-deposit-back-from-your-landlord/)).
- Lock-in clauses of 3–12 months make tenants liable for the remaining months' rent if they leave early ([RentenPe](https://rentenpe.com/blog/lockin-period-in-rent-agreements-what-it-means-and-how-to-negotiate)).

### Solution mechanism (step by step)
1. Arun, 24, moving from Madurai for an IT job on OMR, shortlists 2 houses and a PG. For each one he sends a photo of the draft agreement, or a voice note with the terms (rent, advance, maintenance, EB rate, water, lock-in, notice, painting clause).
2. The bot extracts the terms and calculates:
   - the **true monthly cost**: rent + maintenance + EB at the owner's rate for his likely usage, compared with the TN domestic tariff (*current tariff table to be loaded*) + water;
   - the **day-1 cash** he'll need;
   - an estimate of the **exit cost** (painting clause, lock-in).
3. It shows the options side by side, with red flags and a polite negotiation script ("Can we do 3 months advance and EB at the TNEB rate?").
4. Arun chooses and signs on his own. When he moves in, the bot offers vetted packers, broadband and cleaning. Those vendors pay per lead.

### Who saves money, and how much *(all assumptions)*
- EB at a fair rate: ₹400–900 a month for 100–150 units.
- A lower advance frees ₹30k–₹80k of cash (≈ ₹3–8k a year if it would otherwise be borrowed).
- Avoiding an unfair painting clause: ₹5–15k at exit.

### Who pays you, and why
- **Move-in vendors** (packers and movers, broadband, furniture rental, cleaning) pay per lead or job *(assumption; rates unverified)*. They want new movers at exactly the moment they move.
- This is a crowded lead-generation market where NoBroker already monetises the same moment, so revenue is uncertain.

### F1–F6 check
| Filter | Pass? | Reason | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | ✅ | Paper agreements or spoken terms via voice note; cash advance is fine | 4 |
| F2 | ✅ | Free for tenants; vendors pay | 4 |
| F3 | ⚠️ Pass (pre-signing only) | The tenant alone chooses which house and what to ask for. *After* signing, disputes need the landlord or a Rent Authority/court, so that is **out of scope** | 3 |
| F4 | ⚠️ Weak | AI agreement reviewers: [Contract Shield](https://contractshield.in/) (free to start, flags rent red flags), [eDrafter Rent Agreement AI](https://www.edrafter.in/rent-agreement-ai/), [goHeather](https://www.goheather.io/ai-document-review/rental-agreement-ai-review); legal services [LegalKart](https://www.legalkart.com/en/legal-blog/new-rent-rules-2025-explained-online-registration-&-deposit-limits-made-simple), [LegalDesk](https://legaldesk.com/rental-agreement/new-rent-law-2025); platforms [NoBroker](https://www.nobroker.in/blog/mistakes-to-avoid-in-rental-agreement/), [RentOk](https://rentok.com/blogs/market-trends/rental-laws-in-india-you-should-know-in-2025-state-by-state), [Housewise](https://housewise.in/blog/custom-rent-agreement-clauses-india/); deposit protection [Unetra](https://www.unetra.co.in/); Chennai community [Verified.RealEstate](https://community.verified.realestate/article/new-rental-rules-2025-big-wins-for-both-tenants-and-landlords/) | 2 |
| F5 | ⚠️ Weak | Easy to build (2–3 weeks), but the revenue model is unclear and competes with NoBroker | 2 |
| F6 | ✅ | New-joiner groups at IT parks, PG WhatsApp groups, r/Chennai, Tamil reels about "10 months advance?" | 4 |

### Competitors and the gap
The players are listed under F4. **The remaining gap:** a check in Tamil on WhatsApp that turns TN-specific *money* terms (EB per-unit rate, advance months, maintenance, painting clause) into one true-cost number and compares houses side by side. The AI contract tools review legal wording, not rupees, but they could close this gap quickly.

### Biggest reason it could fail
**Tenants have little bargaining power** in tight Chennai and Bengaluru rental markets. Knowing a term is unfair doesn't mean the tenant can change it, so the savings may not happen and the payer (vendor leads) is weak.

### 2-week validation test
- Collect terms from **30 renters** (new-joiner groups, PG groups, r/Chennai, r/bangalore). Follow up after 7 days.
- **Keep going if:** ≥ 30% changed a term or chose a cheaper-to-live option because of the check; **≥ 3 move-in vendors** agree to pay per lead.
- **Stop otherwise.**

### MVP (2–3 weeks)
- WhatsApp bot with LLM extraction of terms from photos and voice notes.
- EB calculator (TN domestic tariff table).
- True-cost and day-1 cash calculator.
- Red-flag rules.
- Side-by-side comparison of up to 3 options.
- Vendor referral links.

## 6. Notes for Level 2

1. **#1 vs Sedharam Check.**
   - **Where #1 is weaker:** smaller saving per purchase (₹3–10k vs ₹13k+ per sovereign, both partly assumptions), and a thinner-margin payer (two-wheeler dealers make 3–6% vs jewellers' making charges).
   - **Where #1 is stronger:** a standardised item, which makes comparison clean; statutory lines that can be calculated exactly, which makes the "honest number" indisputable; a younger, more digital audience; and fresh 2026 news hooks (the IRDAI draft, GST 2.0 aftermath, the EV road-tax exemption).
   - **The engine is shared.** Level 2 could test both in parallel at little extra cost.
2. **Must verify before committing to #1:**
   - (a) whether dealers will pay per sale (the main risk);
   - (b) the final status of the two-helmet rule;
   - (c) the TN Government Order on EV road-tax exemption for 2026–27;
   - (d) IRDAI rules on any insurance referral;
   - (e) current WhatsApp Business Platform pricing.
3. **Extension path:** two-wheeler purchase (#1) → service estimates (#2) → cars. For cars, dealer insurance and handling are much larger ([example: ₹53k vs ₹25k insurance](https://www.theswipeup.com/2026/08/no-car-or-showroom-insurance-what.html)), and MyCarHelpline shows people use this kind of help.
4. **Lessons from domain (b):** most "new 2024–26 problems" either need a government office (labour codes, PF, PM-JAY), have no payer (tariff hikes, deepfake scams), or were crowded within months (quick-commerce comparison, EV battery repair). New *regulatory changes* work best as **marketing hooks and credibility** for a bill-check product rather than as products themselves.
5. **Passed on to other scouts:** wedding silk-saree price and purity check (Kanchipuram); chit-fund effective-rate check (household finance).
