# Scout B: small business owners and the self-employed (Tamil Nadu / India)

**Level 1 research scout report** · 28 Sep 2026
**Domain:** kirana shops, eateries (hotels, messes, tiffin centres), bakeries and sweet shops, tailors, salons, mechanics, small manufacturers and job-work units (Tiruppur, Coimbatore, Erode, Salem, Sivakasi), clinics, labs and pharmacies, tuition centres, auto, taxi and lorry owners, street vendors, and home businesses. In all of them **the owner is the only decision-maker**.

> **Method note.** I brainstormed 26 raw problems, killed 23 of them against F1–F6, and ran web searches on every idea that survived the first pass. The session's shared web-search budget ran out near the end, so a few supporting facts below are marked **"unverified"** or **"from search summary"**. Every number that is my own estimate is marked **(assumption)**.

**What I learned about this domain:**
- **Cost-cutting ideas on the buying side are crowded here.** Funded players already cover them: Khatabook and OkCredit for credit, Udaan, Jumbotail and JioMart Partner for kirana stock, Hyperpure for restaurant supplies, Petpooja and UrbanPiper for Swiggy/Zomato reconciliation, and Kirana Club for trade schemes.
- **The open space is on the "earn more" and "sudden new cost" side**, where prices changed so fast in 2025–26 that owners haven't caught up:
  - coconut shells were repriced 4–7× in a year;
  - commercial LPG went through a price and supply shock in 2026.
- **Ideas 1 and 2 serve the same customer** (TN tiffin centres, messes and hotels) and could run as one WhatsApp relationship: *"earn from your shells, save on your gas"* (see §5).

## 2. Idea 1: **Sirattai Kaasu** (coconut shells → cash)

**Pitch:** *"Your hotel's coconut shells now sell for ₹20+ a kilo. Send 'சிரட்டை' on WhatsApp for today's fair rate, and we weigh and pay cash at your door every week."*

### The problem, with evidence
- **Shells have been repriced sharply.**
  - In Kerala, shells sold for **₹9/kg in 2024 and more than ₹32/kg by Aug 2025** ([IANS via Prokerala](https://www.prokerala.com/news/articles/a1665060.html)). They were **₹5/kg at the start of 2025 and up to ₹35/kg** later that year ([NewKerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/o/once-waste-gold-demand-drives-coconut-shell-prices-record-266), [The Commune](https://thecommunemag.com/the-new-gold-keralas-coconut-shells-turn-lucrative-commodity-as-prices-soar-to-%E2%82%B9-35-kg/)).
  - The shells go to **charcoal factories in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka**, and TN buyers travel to Kerala to collect them. Kerala households have started storing shells to sell ([Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/kerala-news/coconut-shell-prices-skyrocket-in-kerala-surpassing-coconut-rates-articleshow-ygnnwi9)).
- **The same is happening in Tamil Nadu.** In the Pollachi area a tonne of shells reached **₹29,000 in May 2026** ([Dinamalar Coimbatore](https://www.dinamalar.com/news/tamil-nadu-district-news-coimbatore/coconut-husk-price-hits-record-high-at-rs-29000-per-tonne-price-hike-due-to-lack-of-supply/3905039)) and **₹34,500 later in 2026**, against **about ₹8,500 a year earlier** ([Dinamalar](https://www.dinamalar.com/news/tamil-nadu-district-news-coimbatore/-fast-rising-/4282552); the year-earlier figure comes from a search summary and is **unverified**). The reason given is too few shells arriving.
- **Buyers are short of supply.**
  - *BusinessLine* (9 Sep 2026): "Coconut shell price surge threatens India's activated carbon exports" ([PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/businessline-mumbai-9wvz/20260909/281861535386388)).
  - Exporters of activated carbon (a ₹4,700 crore export market) are asking the government for help with the shell price ([Dinamalar business](https://www.dinamalar.com/news/business-general-news/coconut-syrup-prices-continue-to-rise-exporters-seeking-government-help-carbon-export-market-rs-4700-crore/4317165)).
- **The volume is big.**
  - **Chennai alone uses over 3 lakh coconuts a day** ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/chennai/chennai-coconuts-touch-unreachable-rates-as-supply-dips-838573)).
  - One shell weighs about **0.25 ± 0.08 kg** for hybrid nuts ([ResearchGate table](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Measured-values-on-weight-of-whole-coconut-husks-shells-and-copra-juice-of-hybrid-and_tbl1_341137302)), roughly 30% of a dehusked nut ([Tetra Pak Coconut Handbook](https://coconuthandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/composition), from search summary).
  - Using a conservative **0.15–0.2 kg per shell (assumption)**, Chennai produces **45–60 tonnes of shells a day, worth about ₹11–15 lakh a day at ₹25/kg (assumption)**. Most of that comes from households, but a large share comes from eateries.
- **Eateries are squeezed at the same moment.** Coconuts cost ₹58–60/kg wholesale and ₹80 retail in Chennai, and some small eateries swapped coconut chutney for mint chutney ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/chennai/chennai-coconuts-touch-unreachable-rates-as-supply-dips-838573)). LPG also rose to ₹2,916 a cylinder (Idea 2). Money from shells directly offsets these costs.
- **Chennai retail listings show ₹20–21/kg** for shells ([IndiaMART listing](https://m.indiamart.com/temple-exports/new-items.html)).
- **Still unverified: what TN eateries do with their shells today.** They might bin them, give them to staff, burn them, or sell them to a collector at an unknown rate. That's exactly what the 2-week test checks.

### How a real owner uses it (illustrative)
Murugan runs a 40-seat tiffin centre in Karur and breaks about 25 coconuts a day for chutney.
1. He sees a Tamil reel, or a flyer handed over by our collector: *"உங்க கடை சிரட்டை – கிலோ ₹__. இன்றைய ரேட் WhatsApp-ல் கேளுங்க."* ("Your shop's coconut shells: ₹__ a kilo. Ask for today's rate on WhatsApp.") His son sends "சிரட்டை" to the number.
2. The bot replies with a Tamil voice note giving **this week's fair rate for Karur** (the buyers' delivered price minus transport and our margin) and a **fixed weekly pickup day**.
3. Murugan's staff put shells into the two free sacks our collector left, with a tip sheet: keep them dry, keep them out of the garbage bin, and scrape out leftover coconut for a better grade.
4. On pickup day the collector weighs the sacks on a **digital hanging scale in front of him** and pays **cash**. An automatic **WhatsApp receipt** (kg × rate = ₹) goes to the son's phone, so there's no weighing dispute.
5. The collector's route of 30–60 eateries fills a goods auto. Once there are **2–3 tonnes**, a truck takes them to a shell-charcoal or activated-carbon unit in the Pollachi–Kangeyam–Udumalpet belt.
6. Later phases:
   - **kiranas become drop points** for household shells, weighing them on their own scales and earning a margin;
   - the same weekly visit collects **used cooking oil for Ecoil/Uranus** as a paid referral instead of competing with them.

### Who saves or earns, and how much (all figures are assumptions unless linked)
| Eatery type | Coconuts/day | Shells/month | Paid to eatery at ₹15–25/kg | For context |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small tiffin centre | 15–30 | 70–180 kg | **₹1,000–4,500/month** | ≈ 0.3–1.5 commercial cylinders at [₹2,916.50](https://www.goodreturns.in/lpg-price-in-tamil-nadu-s30.html) |
| Mid-size hotel or mess | 50–150 | 225–900 kg | **₹3,400–22,500/month** | ≈ 1–8 cylinders |

**Our side (assumption):** a margin of ₹3–6/kg on 2–4 tonnes a week per town route gives about **₹25,000–1 lakh a month per route**. The margin rests on the buyer's delivered price of about ₹29–34.5/kg (Pollachi figure, Dinamalar) minus the price paid to eateries and ₹2–4/kg for transport.

### Who pays us, and why
- **Shell-charcoal and activated-carbon units** pay either a **delivered price** (we keep the spread) or a **per-kg sourcing fee**.
- **Why they would:** they're short of raw material. Arrivals are low, exporters are lobbying about it, and BusinessLine says the shell price threatens exports. Steady, dry, sorted urban supply is worth paying for.
- **The eatery pays nothing.** It simply gets money for something it treated as rubbish.

### Competitors and why the gap still exists
- **Informal shell traders and charcoal-unit agents** ([IndiaMART Pollachi](https://dir.indiamart.com/pollachi/coconut-shell.html), [Coimbatore](https://dir.indiamart.com/coimbatore/coconut-shell.html)). They buy in bulk from farms, copra dryers and oil mills, not from hundreds of small urban kitchens, and they don't publish a fair rate.
- **General scrap-pickup apps** ([Aakri](https://aakri.in/aakri-impact/about-us/), Scrapuncle, The Kabadiwala; [scraprates.in](https://scraprates.in/chennai) lists metals, plastic and cardboard but not shells). They focus on metros, households and metal or paper, and have no shell grade or route.
- **Upcyclers** ([Thenga](https://www.thengacoco.com/en-us)) take tiny volumes for crafts.
- **Why the gap exists:** shells were worth ₹5–9/kg until 2024, so nobody built urban collection. The **4–7× repricing happened in the last 12–18 months**, faster than informal networks have reached eateries in non-coconut districts.

### Biggest reason it could fail
**It's a commodity cycle.** Shell prices jumped because coconut output fell ([BusinessLine](https://www.pressreader.com/india/businessline-mumbai-9wvz/20260909/281861535386388)). If yields recover and the price slides back towards ₹10/kg, the spread no longer covers collection costs.

Two secondary risks:
- informal collectors may already pay eateries a fair price near the Kongu belt;
- during LPG shortages eateries may **burn their own shells** (hotels moved to firewood and charcoal in March 2026, per [India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/tamil-nadu/chennai-induction-stove-firewood-how-hotels-in-chennai-switch-to-alternative-methods-amid-lpg-shortage-2026-03-14-1033759)).

### 2-week validation test
**Where:** one town 60–150 km from the Pollachi–Kangeyam belt, such as Karur, Erode, Dindigul or Tiruppur.
- **Days 1–4:** visit 50 eateries and sweet shops. Ask how many coconuts they use a day, where the shells go now, and at what price. Phone 6 shell-charcoal units from IndiaMART listings to get **delivered-price quotes** and ask whether they'd pay a sourcing fee.
- **Days 5–14:** sign up eateries, run **two pickup rounds** with a hired goods auto and a ₹2,000 hanging scale, and sell the lot to the best buyer.

**Keep going if all of these hold:**
- ≥ 60% of eateries currently get **nothing or ≤ ₹10/kg**;
- ≥ 25 sign up;
- ≥ 1 tonne is collected in the 2 weeks;
- **net spread ≥ ₹3/kg** after transport;
- ≥ 1 buyer commits to weekly purchases.

**Stop if any of these happens:**
- most eateries already get ≥ ₹20/kg;
- fewer than 10 sign up;
- the net spread is under ₹1.5/kg.

### MVP (one developer, 2–3 weeks, about ₹25–40k including the float)
- **WhatsApp bot** (Cloud API, or the free WhatsApp Business app with broadcast lists at first) with Tamil voice replies. It handles today's rate by town, pickup booking ("how many sacks?"), and weekly reminders.
- **Collector web form** on a phone: shop, kg, rate, then an automatic WhatsApp receipt to the owner.
- **Route sheet:** eateries due today, ordered by Google Maps link.
- **Ledger:** kg bought, kg sold, buyer price, spread and transport per trip.
- **Price admin:** buyer quotes in, town rate out.
- **Stack:** Node or Python, Supabase or Google Sheets. Hardware: a digital scale (₹1.5–3k), sacks, and a hired goods auto at ₹1,000–1,500 a day (assumption).

## 3. Idea 2: **Gas Kanakku** (LPG spend check for eateries, paid only from savings)

**Pitch:** *"At ₹2,916 a cylinder, is your kitchen burning more gas than it should? Log each cylinder change with one WhatsApp tap. We compare you with similar eateries, fix the leaks and bad burners for free, and you pay a share only if your cylinders really last longer."*

### The problem, with evidence
- **Price shock.**
  - A 19 kg commercial cylinder in Chennai costs **₹2,916.50 (22 Sep 2026)** ([Goodreturns TN](https://www.goodreturns.in/lpg-price-in-tamil-nadu-s30.html)).
  - In May 2026 it jumped **by about ₹990–1,013 in one revision**, from ₹2,246.50 to about ₹3,237–3,259 ([LiveChennai](https://www.livechennai.com/detailnews.asp?catid=&newsid=79999)).
  - Chennai hotels say the rise "significantly escalated daily expenses, particularly for small and mid-sized establishments" ([News Today](https://newstodaynet.com/2026/05/02/lpg-price-hike-hits-chennai-hotels-hard/)).
  - PG and hostel rents went up because of gas costs ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/chennai/chennai-rising-gas-prices-push-pgs-hostels-to-hike-room-rents-from-may-5)).
- **Supply shock.**
  - In March 2026 hotels in **Chennai, Madurai, Coimbatore and tier-2 TN towns** warned they might shut ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2026/03/11/lpg-shortage-chennai-hotels-rethink-menus-threaten-gig-economy.html), [South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/lpg-shortage-hits-tamil-nadu-hotels-struggle-auto-rickshaws-off-roads-as-commercial-cylinder-supply-stops/)).
  - Idli prices went from ₹10 to ₹15–20 ([LiveChennai](https://www.livechennai.com/detailnews.asp?newsid=79513)), and kitchens switched to firewood and induction ([India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/tamil-nadu/chennai-induction-stove-firewood-how-hotels-in-chennai-switch-to-alternative-methods-amid-lpg-shortage-2026-03-14-1033759), [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/tamil-nadu/food-prices-set-to-increase-as-hotels-shift-to-firewood-others-biryani-takes-the-hit-during-eid-3939702)).
- **Much of the gas is avoidable waste**, going by these claims, which the pilot must verify:
  - ISI stoves with ≥ 68% thermal efficiency save up to 15% of gas ([SuperGas](https://www.supergas.com/for-hotel/advice-on-saving-energy)).
  - Typical commercial burners run at **36–45%** efficiency, against **69%** for Agnisumukh's certified burner (tested by the LPG Equipment Research Centre of IOCL/BPCL/HPCL), with about **30% fuel saving** claimed ([Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/106424/agnisumukh-energy-efficient-stoves-hari-rao-it-officer/), [SEforALL](https://www.seforall.org/stories-of-success/agnisumukhs-clean-cooking-india)).
  - One restaurant-software blog says clogged burners waste 20% and pressure cookers halve the gas for dal and rice ([DineCard](https://www.dinecard.in/blog/restaurant-lpg-cylinder-consumption-calculator-india), a vendor claim).
- **Owners can't tell whether their usage is high.** Nobody benchmarks "kg of LPG per 100 plates". Owners count cylinders only when paying the bill, and the market is full of doubtful "gas saver" gadgets *(general knowledge)*.

### How a real owner uses it (illustrative)
Selvi runs a mess in Madurai that uses about one cylinder a day.
1. Her nephew sends "கேஸ்" to the number after seeing a reel: *"ஒரு சிலிண்டர் ₹2,916 – உங்க கடையில எத்தனை நாள் வருது?"* ("One cylinder costs ₹2,916. How many days does one last in your shop?")
2. The bot asks, in Tamil voice notes with buttons:
   - how many cylinders were bought last month (from the distributor's paper bills);
   - the main items (idli/dosa, meals, parotta, tea, frying);
   - rough plates a day;
   - the number of burners and their type.
3. She gets **one comparable number**: estimated **kg of LPG per 100 plates**, set against a benchmark for similar eateries. It says, for example, *"You use about 25% more than similar messes"*. The benchmark starts from literature and our own first audits, so it's marked as an estimate until the data grows.
4. **Cylinder diary.** Whenever a new cylinder is connected, the staff tap **"புது சிலிண்டர்"** ("new cylinder"), or tick a paper tally card on the wall and send a photo of it weekly. Once a day they tap **"கூட்டம்: குறைவு / சாதா / அதிகம்"** ("rush: low / normal / high"), so busy days don't distort the result.
5. **Free fixes first.** The bot asks for a **photo of each burner's flame** (a yellow flame means a clogged burner or a bad air mix), then gives a Tamil checklist:
   - clean the burner heads;
   - check for leaks with soapy water;
   - use lids;
   - pressure-cook rice and dal;
   - soak dal;
   - switch off idle burners.

   A partner technician visits if needed.
6. If the gap remains, we offer a **high-efficiency burner retrofit through a vendor partner** or an induction unit for boiling jobs. **Selvi pays nothing upfront.** She pays **25% of the measured savings for 6 months**, taken from the diary (assumption for the terms).
7. A **monthly WhatsApp card** shows, for example, *"This month: 6 fewer cylinders = ₹17,500 saved; our share ₹4,375."*

### Who saves money, and how much (assumptions except the price)
| Eatery | Cylinders/month | Spend at ₹2,916.50 | 10–20% saving (assumption) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small café or tea stall | 4–6 ([DineCard](https://www.dinecard.in/blog/restaurant-lpg-cylinder-consumption-calculator-india)) | ₹11,700–17,500 | **₹1,200–3,500/month** |
| Mess or tiffin centre | ~30 | ~₹87,500 | **₹8,750–17,500/month** |
| Medium restaurant | 15–25 ([DineCard](https://www.dinecard.in/blog/restaurant-lpg-cylinder-consumption-calculator-india)) | ₹43,700–72,900 | **₹4,400–14,600/month** |

**Our side (assumption):** a mess saving 15% of ₹87,500 (₹13,100 a month) pays a 25% share, about **₹3,300 a month**, or **about ₹19,700 over 6 months**. With 30 such eateries that's about ₹1 lakh a month, plus about 10% commission on burner or induction sales.

### Who pays us, and why
1. **The eatery, only out of verified savings.** F2 explicitly allows this. It will pay because it takes no risk (no saving, no fee) and because the diary makes the saving visible in its own cylinder count.
2. **Efficient-burner and induction vendors** pay a referral commission. After March 2026 they have demand but no trusted way to reach small eateries in towns: [Mongabay](https://india.mongabay.com/2026/05/lpg-shortage-leads-to-increased-demand-for-newer-clean-cooking-solutions/) reports rising demand for clean-cooking solutions, and Agnisumukh's customers are big kitchens such as ITC Hotels, Infosys and Apollo.

### F1–F6 check
| Filter | Pass? | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 Ground reality | ✅ | 4 | Cylinders are physical and counted anyway. A paper tally card plus one WhatsApp user is enough. The cooks' cooperation is needed |
| F2 No new cost | ✅ | 4 | The check and free fixes cost nothing, and retrofits are paid only from measured savings. Measuring savings fairly is the hard part |
| F3 No gatekeepers | ✅ | 5 | The owner decides alone. Cleaning or swapping a burner needs no approval (avoid bulk LPG installations over 100 kg, which fall under the explosives regulator, *unverified threshold*) |
| F4 Not crowded | ⚠️ | 3 | [Agnisumukh](https://agnisumukh.com/) (big kitchens), burner makers, the [IIT-Guwahati stove](https://thebetterindia.com/264513/assam-iit-guwahati-innovation-cheap-low-cost-lpg-new-cooking-stove/) and LPG-survival blogs ([Menumanager](https://menumanager.in/lpg-shortage-restaurant-survival-guide/), [DineOpen](https://www.dineopen.com/blog/lpg-gas-crisis-restaurants-india-2026.html)) exist. **No neutral benchmark-and-pay-from-savings service for small eateries was found** |
| F5 Practical for founder | ⚠️ | 3 | The bot and diary are easy. It needs a trustworthy technician partner and careful safety practice. Savings are noisy because daily sales vary |
| F6 Cheap to market | ✅ | 5 | Every TN hotel owner is talking about gas prices. Tamil reels, hotel owners' WhatsApp groups and door-to-door visits all work |

### Competitors found, and why the gap still exists
- **Agnisumukh** (Bangalore): about 500 commercial kitchens, high-end clients ([Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/106424/agnisumukh-energy-efficient-stoves-hari-rao-it-officer/)). It sells hardware and doesn't benchmark or measure savings for a ₹3-lakh-a-month mess.
- **Burner and stove makers and dealers** ([IndiaMART Madurai](https://dir.indiamart.com/madurai/single-burner-gas-stove.html), [The Hotel Mart](https://www.tradeindia.com/products/triple-burner-gas-stove-3686531.html)) sell products with unverified claims.
- **LPG companies' advice pages** ([SuperGas](https://www.supergas.com/for-hotel/advice-on-saving-energy)) and **restaurant-software blogs** offer generic tips, with no measurement and no accountability.
- **Why the gap exists:** until 2026 gas was 3–5% of revenue ([DineCard](https://www.dinecard.in/blog/restaurant-lpg-cylinder-consumption-calculator-india)) and not worth attention. Since April 2026 the price has risen about 30% (₹2,246.50 → ₹2,916.50), on top of an earlier ₹302.50 rise this year and a physical shortage in March. Before that, measured savings weren't worth selling.

### Biggest reason it could fail
**Savings may be too small or too noisy to prove.** If real kitchens save only 5–8%, or daily sales swings hide the effect, owners will dispute the share and stop paying. Also, if the Gulf supply situation eases and prices fall back, urgency fades. And one bad technician visit involving gas is a **safety and reputation risk**.

### 2-week validation test
**Where and who:** 12 eateries in one town that each use **at least one cylinder every two days**, so that feedback is fast.
- **Days 1–3:** record baseline cylinders from the last month's paper bills, start the diary and the rush-level taps, collect flame photos, and do free checks (cleaning, leaks, lids, pressure cooker).
- **Days 4–14:** keep logging, and compare plates per cylinder (adjusted for rush level) with the baseline.

**Keep going if:**
- ≥ 8 of 12 keep logging for 14 days;
- ≥ 5 show ≥ 10% improvement;
- ≥ 3 sign a one-page pay-from-savings agreement or buy an efficient burner through us.

**Stop if:**
- the median improvement is under 5%;
- fewer than 5 log consistently;
- no owner will sign a savings-share agreement.

### MVP (one developer, 2–3 weeks, about ₹15–25k)
- **WhatsApp bot** with Tamil voice prompts and buttons: onboarding questions, cylinder diary, daily rush level, and flame-photo upload (reviewed manually at first).
- **Benchmark calculator:** kg of LPG per 100 plates by eatery type. A seed table (assumption) is updated from our own audits.
- **Savings engine:** baseline versus current days per cylinder, adjusted for rush level, which produces the monthly savings card and the invoice for our share.
- **Technician and vendor booking** plus a referral ledger.
- **Kit:** soapy-water leak-test bottle, burner cleaning brushes and pins, printed tally cards.

## 4. Idea 3: **Kazhivu Rate** (fair grade-wise bids for small units' textile cutting waste)

**Pitch:** *"Small cutting, stitching and job-work units send a photo and weight of their cutting waste and get this week's grade-wise bids from verified recyclers, plus a Tamil guide to which sorting earns more, instead of taking one broker's lump price for mixed waste."*

### The problem, with evidence
- **A fragmented, broker-driven trade.**
  - CEEW says India's pre-consumer textile waste moves through "decentralised networks of brokers, traders and informal intermediaries". Multiple handovers raise costs and make "material quality and final destinations" hard to track ([CEEW](https://www.ceew.in/publications/pre-consumer-textile-waste-management-india)).
  - Tiruppur is one of the main recycling clusters (same source).
  - The ecosystem "has been running quietly for forty years" ([Zerodha Daily Brief](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/india-textile-waste-problem-gwalior-telecom-hardware)).
- **Grade matters a lot.**
  - Tiruppur listings price **coloured banian waste at about ₹27–33/kg and white at ₹95–116/kg** ([IndiaMART](https://dir.indiamart.com/tiruppur/banyan-waste.html)). These are resale listing prices, not what units receive.
  - Clean cotton cuttings from a Tiruppur exporter reportedly fetch **₹28–32/kg from a Panipat garnetting mill** (from a search summary of CEEW-related coverage, **unverified**).
  - So sorting white from coloured, and cotton from blends, plausibly changes the value several times over.
- **Small units lose out.** Formal programmes target big exporters. The Tiruppur Exporters' Association works with Reverse Resources ([Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/news/sustainability-news/tiruppur-gears-up-for-next-phase-of-growth-journey-301282-newsdetails.htm), [H&M Foundation](https://hmfoundation.com/2021/09/15/shifting-the-industry-through-waste-management/)), and Fashion for Good's pilot runs with brands like adidas and Levi's ([Fashion for Good](https://www.fashionforgood.com/case-study/sorting-for-circularity-india/)). Small non-export units aren't part of them.
- **Unverified:** the actual gap between what small units receive and the best bid for the same grade. The test in this section measures it.

### How a real owner uses it (illustrative)
Ravi runs a 20-machine stitching and cutting job-work unit in Tiruppur's outskirts.
1. He sends a photo of his waste sacks and "about 60 kg a day, mostly cotton, mixed colours" to the bot.
2. The bot replies with the **grade** (mixed cotton, coloured) and **this week's best bid** (₹X/kg from verified buyer A). It adds: *"If you keep white and coloured in separate sacks, whites get ₹Y."*
3. Ravi's helpers use **two colour-coded sacks** that we provide free.
4. When he (or three neighbouring units together) reaches half a tonne, the winning buyer picks up and weighs on site. Payment is **cash or NEFT, whichever he prefers**, and a WhatsApp receipt shows the grade, kg and rate.

### Who saves or earns, and how much (assumptions)
- A small unit cutting 300–1,000 kg of fabric a day with 10–20% cutting waste produces **0.9–6 tonnes of waste a month** (assumption; **the waste percentage is unverified**).
- An uplift of **₹3–8/kg** from sorting and competing bids is worth **₹2,700–48,000 a month** per unit (assumption).
- **Our side:** a sourcing fee of ₹0.5–1/kg from buyers. At 150 tonnes a month from 50 units that's **₹75,000–1.5 lakh a month** (assumption).

### Who pays us, and why
**Recyclers pay a per-kg sourcing fee.** That means garnetting mills, open-end spinners using recycled fibre, and wiping-cloth traders. They want **consistent, pre-sorted feedstock** and traceability that they can pass to brands chasing recycled-content targets (the pressure behind Reverse Resources and Fashion for Good). **Units pay nothing.**

### F1–F6 check
| Filter | Pass? | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 Ground reality | ✅ | 4 | Deals are already in cash and by weight. The unit owner or supervisor uses WhatsApp photos, and receipts can be paper |
| F2 No new cost | ✅ | 5 | Units earn more, and buyers pay the fee |
| F3 No gatekeepers | ✅ | 5 | The unit owner decides alone. No association, officer or exporter approval is needed |
| F4 Not crowded | ⚠️ | 3 | [Reverse Resources](https://www.reverseresources.net/) is already in Tiruppur through the exporters' association. [MuddleArt](https://www.muddleart.com/) handles 7,000+ t but is Delhi-based (Okhla). Both, plus brokers and IndiaMART, serve exporters and large units, not small TN job-work units |
| F5 Practical for founder | ⚠️ | 3 | The bot is easy. Recruiting 5+ reliable buyers, grading disputes and aggregating loads are hard |
| F6 Cheap to market | ⚠️ | 3 | Tiruppur WhatsApp groups and word of mouth work, but it's B2B and relationship-driven: fewer owners to reach, and a slower yes |

### Competitors and why the gap still exists
- **Reverse Resources** ([site](https://www.reverseresources.net/)) is a tracking and trading platform tied to brands, used by TEA members. It's built for exporters with brand contracts.
- **MuddleArt** ([site](https://www.muddleart.com/), [AIC-IIITH](https://aic.iiit.ac.in/muddleart-creating-dignified-livelihoods-by-maximizing-textile-waste-value/)) collects and sorts in NCR.
- **Local waste merchants** ([IndiaMART Tiruppur](https://dir.indiamart.com/tiruppur/textile-waste.html), [cotton waste dealers](https://www.indianyellowpages.com/tirupur/cotton-waste.htm)) buy mixed lots, give advances, and publish no rates.
- **Why the gap exists:** formal players follow brand money, which sits with exporters. Small job-work units produce smaller, mixed lots with no traceability demands, so nobody gives them grade-wise price discovery.

### Biggest reason it could fail
**The gap may not really exist.** A 40-year-old broker network may already pay near-fair prices for mixed waste, and the "uplift" may just be the cost of the helpers' time spent sorting. Brokers also give **advances and instant pickup**, which small units value more than ₹2/kg.

### 2-week validation test
- **Days 1–7:** interview 30 small non-export units in Tiruppur about monthly waste kg, grade, current buyer, price and advance terms. Take 5 sample photos per grade.
- **Days 5–14:** get written weekly bids for the same grades from 5 buyers: 2 garnetting or recycling mills, 2 wiping-cloth traders and 1 open-end spinner. Run 3 trial sales.

**Keep going if:**
- the median gap is **≥ ₹4/kg** for the same grade;
- ≥ 10 units agree to a trial sale;
- ≥ 2 buyers agree in writing to a sourcing fee.

**Stop if:**
- the gap is under ₹2/kg;
- units refuse because of broker advances or tie-ins.

### MVP (one developer, 2–3 weeks, about ₹10–20k)
- **WhatsApp intake:** photo, estimated kg, and a composition picker (cotton / blend / poly, white / coloured).
- **Grade guide:** Tamil photo cards showing what each grade looks like.
- **Weekly bid sheet:** buyers enter bids per grade in a form, and units receive the best bid by broadcast.
- **Pickup pooling:** nearby units combine to make a half-tonne load.
- **Receipts** and a **buyer-fee ledger**.

## 5. Notes for the Level 2 lead

1. **Ideas 1 and 2 could merge into one eatery product, "Kitchen Kanakku".**
   - The same TN tiffin centre earns money from shells and saves on gas. The weekly shell pickup is a free, trusted field visit that can also collect the cylinder tally card.
   - One WhatsApp relationship would carry **two revenue lines**: shell spread from buyers and a share of gas savings.
   - This eases idea 1's thin-software problem and idea 2's trust problem.
2. **How they compare with the front-runner (Sedharam Check):**
   - All three copy its good pattern: a WhatsApp input, one comparable number, and a third party or savings paying.
   - Sirattai Kaasu and Kazhivu Rate involve **physical logistics**, which Sedharam doesn't.
   - Gas Kanakku involves **safety** (LPG).
   - Sedharam is the purer software play. Sirattai Kaasu likely makes money faster, because a shell sale earns on day 1 while a jewellery referral waits for a purchase.
3. **Timing risk:**
   - Ideas 1 and 2 ride 2025–26 shocks: the coconut shortage and the Gulf LPG disruption.
   - Level 2 should stress-test what happens if shell prices halve or LPG falls back to early-2026 levels.
   - If the delivered shell price halves to about ₹15/kg, Idea 1 works only if collection plus transport stays under about ₹3/kg and eateries accept about ₹8–10/kg (assumption).
4. **Facts to verify before any go decision** (I ran out of search budget):
   - how many coconuts eateries use a day;
   - what TN eateries do with their shells today;
   - the Pollachi price trend after July 2026;
   - real LPG savings in TN dosa and idli kitchens;
   - the cutting-waste percentage and the price gap for small Tiruppur units.
