# Sirattai Kaasu (சிரட்டை காசு): launch kit

**Level 4 marketing** · written 29 Sep 2026 · covers 1 Oct to 29 Dec 2026
**Built on:** `research/level3/decision.md`, `research/level1/scout-b-small-business.md` (Idea 1), `research/level2/v1-redteam.md` (§3, §8) and `research/level2/v2-market.md`.

## How to use this kit
- **Weeks 1–2:** use §3 (validation playbook). Print only the validation items in §8 and spend nothing else until the keep/stop decision on **Thu 15 Oct**.
- **If the decision is GO:** use §4 (copy), §5 (trust) and §6 (calendar), and track §7 (metrics).
- **Every piece of copy is in Tamil with English next to it.** The Tamil is written the way shop owners talk. **Have a native Tamil speaker from the pilot town proofread all Tamil before printing or posting.** Local words differ between Karur, Madurai, Salem and so on.

### Labels used here
| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[A]** | An assumption, either from the research reports or mine. Not a checked fact |
| **[unverified]** / **[attr?]** | Carried over from the reports: not confirmed, or the source page is unclear |
| **[check]** | Something the founder must confirm locally before relying on it |
| **[₹__]** | A rate to fill in **only** from the weekly rate engine |

### Three rules that override everything else
1. **Never promise a fixed price.** Every [₹__] comes from the weekly rate engine. Never print a rate on anything that lasts longer than a week (sacks, tags, banners, reels). Never say "minimum", "guaranteed" or "always".
2. **Don't buy shells before a buyer has quoted a delivered price in writing** (a WhatsApp message counts). No quote means no pickup.
3. **Don't collect from temples or tender-coconut vendors** (see §2.4).

## 1. Positioning and name

**Positioning (one line)**
> **English:** For eateries in [town] that bin or give away their coconut shells, Sirattai Kaasu is the weekly pickup that comes on a fixed day, weighs the shells in front of the owner and pays cash on the spot with a receipt, at a rate published every week.
>
> **தமிழ்:** [ஊர்] ஹோட்டல், மெஸ், டிபன் கடைகளுக்கு: வாரம் ஒரு fixed நாள்ல வந்து, முதலாளி கண் முன்னாடியே சிரட்டையை எடை போட்டு, அங்கேயே ரொக்கமும் ரசீதும் தர்றோம். ரேட்டை ஒவ்வொரு வாரமும் எல்லாருக்கும் தெரியற மாதிரி போடுறோம்.

**Brand line:** **கண் முன்னாடி எடை, கையில காசு.** (*Weighed before your eyes, cash in your hand.*)
**Headline for flyers and reels:** **சிரட்டை குப்பை இல்லைங்க, காசு!** (*Shells aren't rubbish, they're cash!*)

**What we are not.** We are not a scrap dealer who comes "when he feels like it". We don't bid for temple tenders. We don't sell gas-saving services: the decision says not to bundle Gas Kanakku at launch.

**Why this position.** The decision names our edge over informal traders: **transparency** (a published fair rate and a receipt for every weigh-in) and **reliability** (a fixed day). It is not price. Informal traders already work on about ₹2/kg margins (Palakkad example, v2-market), so we won't win a price war. We win on trust and on showing up.

### Name options
| # | Name | What it says | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sirattai Kaasu (சிரட்டை காசு)**, the default | "Money for shells". Cash first, plain Tamil | Already used in all the research. Easy to say on a call |

**Founder to-do before printing anything in bulk [check]:**
- Search the name on IP India's online trademark public search (ipindia.gov.in).
- Check that the WhatsApp Business display name, an Instagram handle and a domain are free.
- If you plan to register the mark, ask a trademark agent which classes apply. Trading, collection/transport and recycling services are likely candidates, but get this confirmed.
- **Validation materials (weeks 1–2) are low-volume and can go ahead meanwhile.**

## 2. Audience map

### 2.1 Suppliers: who gives us shells, and why they care

**How to estimate an eatery's monthly money [A]:**

> **₹/month ≈ coconuts per day × 30 × 0.15–0.2 kg per shell × this week's rate**

- **Shell weight:** 0.15–0.2 kg per shell is Scout B's conservative assumption. A measured hybrid-nut shell weighs about 0.25 ± 0.08 kg (ResearchGate, via Scout B).
- **Rates:**
  - Scout B's table uses ₹15–25/kg [A].
  - The red team's **base case** pays eateries **₹18/kg** [A]. That is the realistic figure at a buyer price of about ₹29/kg. At ₹25/kg to eateries the business loses money unless buyers pay near the top of their range (see §4.0).
- **Use the money figures below to explain value in person only. Never print them as promises.**

| Supplier | Coconuts/day [A] | Shells/month [A] | ₹/month at ₹15/kg [A] | ₹/month at ₹18/kg (base case) [A] | Why they care | Source of the numbers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Small tiffin centre** (idli, dosa, chutney) | 15–30 | 70–180 kg | ₹1,050–2,700 | ₹1,260–3,240 | Chutney every day, coconuts now costly (Chennai ₹58–60/kg wholesale, DT Next via Scout B). **For context only:** a commercial LPG cylinder was **₹2,916.50** on 22 Sep 2026. Scout B puts a small tiffin centre's shell money at about 0.3–1.5 cylinders a month, and a mid-size hotel's at 1–8 cylinders [A]. "Your shells can pay towards a cylinder" is a relatable line when talking in person. LPG prices change every month, so don't print a cylinder figure.

**Where to find them.** Walk the streets with the most eateries: the bus stand, the market, college and hospital roads, and highway stretches [A]. One collector route is itself the marketing channel (v2-market, F6 = 5).

### 2.2 Who decides, and how to win both sides

The **owner decides alone** (F3 = 4). The **kitchen staff** decide whether shells actually end up in the sack. A hidden risk: staff may treat shells as their perk (decision.md, risk 3).

| Person | What they care about | How we win them |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner (முதலாளி)** | Money with no hassle; not being cheated; no mess in the kitchen | Cash on the spot, a receipt, the same day every week, free sacks and one rate for every shop (no haggling) |
| **Coconut breaker / kitchen master (மாஸ்டர், helper)**, who fills the sack | Extra work. Loses out if they take or sell the shells today | Put the sack right where coconuts are broken. Pay a **clean-sack bonus of [₹__] per pickup**, paid only when the sack is dry and holds shells alone, **with the owner's OK and printed on the receipt**. Put their name on the receipt |
| **Cashier, or the owner's son or daughter** | Gets the WhatsApp receipt and the rate message | A short, clear WhatsApp receipt and a weekly rate message; nothing else is sent |

**Staff-share options. Offer both at sign-up and let the owner choose:**
- **Option A, our clean-sack bonus.** We pay the named staff member [₹__] per pickup when the sack is grade 1 (§5.6). This comes out of our spread, so the rate engine must count it as a cost.
  - **Keep it small [A]:** ₹0.5/kg would cut the red team's base-case net of ₹3.7/kg to ₹3.2/kg, just above the ₹3 keep-going line.
  - **It pays for itself only if dry sacks cut our moisture and kernel loss** (10% in the red team's model). Track this (§7).
- **Option B, the owner's own split.** The owner tells us how to split the payment, for example "[₹__] to the master". We pay it separately and write both amounts on the receipt.
- **Never pay staff without the owner knowing.** It would look like we're buying the owner's shells from his own staff.

### 2.3 Buyers: who pays us

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who** | **Shell-charcoal units first** (they burn raw shells). **Activated-carbon plants** only if they buy raw shell and not just shell charcoal. **Ask this on the first call.** All are in the **Pollachi–Kangeyam–Udumalpet belt** |
| **Why they'll buy** | They are short of raw material. Pollachi shells reached **₹29,000/t in May 2026 and ₹34,500/t later in 2026** (Dinamalar, via Scout B). *BusinessLine* (9 Sep 2026) says the shell price surge threatens activated-carbon exports. Exporters in a ₹4,700 crore export market are asking the government for help (Dinamalar). Pollachi nut yields are down 30–60% [attr?], so the shortage should last **at least into December** (market auditor) |
| **What they want** [A: confirm on the call] | Steady weekly lots, dry shells, no stones, sand or rubbish in the sacks, and a predictable delivery day |
| **What we offer** | **1–1.5 t lots, weekly, delivered, for cash on delivery** (decision.md). Eatery shells graded at pickup. A weighbridge slip each time |
| **Who decides** [A] | The unit owner or purchase manager. Ask for the person who fixes the price, not the gate clerk |
| **Where to find them** | IndiaMART listings for Pollachi and Coimbatore coconut-shell dealers (links in Scout B). Ask every unit for referrals |
| **Due diligence** | v2-market notes that Kangeyam taluk has shell-burning units, **some operating illegally** [attr?]. Before the first sale, get the unit's name, address and GST number if it has one, and ask whether it holds pollution-control consent [check which documents to ask for]. Don't sell to a unit you can't identify |
| **Warning** | Kerala traders said in Jan 2026 that availability "has improved". Plan for a **₹2–4/kg** spread, not ₹3–6 (market auditor) |

### 2.4 Who we exclude, and why

| Exclude | Why | What to say if they ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Temples** | Temple shell rights are **tendered and politically contested** (AP example, Deccan Chronicle). TN temples come under **HR&CE**, which brings a lanjam (bribe) risk. That breaks our "no gatekeepers" condition (decision.md) | "மன்னிக்கணும், கோயில் சிரட்டை டெண்டர்ல போறது, அதனால நாங்க எடுக்கிறதில்லைங்க." (*Sorry, temple shells go by tender, so we don't take them.*) |
| **Tender-coconut (இளநீர்) vendors** | They produce **soft green husk, not hard shell**, which is not charcoal feedstock [A, market auditor]. It would also wreck our grade | "இளநீர் மட்டை கரி கம்பெனிக்கு ஆகாதுங்க. கெட்டியான சிரட்டை மட்டும்தான் எடுக்கிறோம்." (*Tender-coconut husk is no use to the charcoal units. We only take hard shells.*) |
| Households (for now) | Too small and too scattered for weekly routes | Later, kirana shops could act as drop points (Scout B, later phase) |

## 3. The 2-week validation playbook (1–14 Oct 2026, decision on Thu 15 Oct)

**Aim.** Find out, before real money is committed, whether:
- eateries get little or nothing for their shells today;
- they will sign up;
- a buyer will pay a delivered price in writing and commit to buying every week;
- the net spread holds.

**The founder, or someone they trust, must attend both pickup rounds and the first buyer sale** (decision.md).

**Town.** The nearest of Karur, Dindigul, Madurai, Trichy, Erode town or Salem. It must be 60–200 km from the buyer belt but outside the coconut-growing belt (decision.md).

**Fixed pickup day for the pilot route: Wednesday.** The weekly rate is published **every Monday**.

### 3.1 Day 0: Wed 30 Sep (preparation)
- Pick **2–3 streets** with the most eateries, then list 60–70 of them to reach 50 interviews.
- Set up the **WhatsApp Business app** (free):
  - business name, hours and town;
  - **quick replies** `/rate`, `/day` and `/scale` (texts in §4.3);
  - **labels**: Surveyed, Signed, Picked-1, Picked-2, Buyer.
- Print or buy:
  - 60 survey sheets (§3.3);
  - 500 A5 flyers with a **blank rate box** (§4.1);
  - 2 numbered duplicate receipt books;
  - 60 sacks and tags (§4.2);
  - 50 kitchen tip cards.
- Have ready: a **digital hanging scale** (₹1.5–3k, in the MVP budget, not marketing) and a **5 kg test weight**.
- Shortlist **6 buyer numbers** from the IndiaMART Pollachi and Coimbatore listings.

**Survey rules**
- **Don't mention our rate until Q5 is answered.** Otherwise owners anchor on our number and the "current price" data is useless.
- Fill in one sheet per eatery and enter it into the validation tracker the same evening.
- If an eatery says it sells by the sack or by the shell, convert to ₹/kg [A]:
  - ₹1 per shell ≈ ₹5–6.7/kg;
  - ₹1.50 per shell ≈ ₹7.5–10/kg.
  - This uses 0.15–0.2 kg per shell. For comparison, Karnataka door-to-door collectors pay ₹1–1.50 per shell (v2-market).

### 3.5 Keep-going and stop thresholds (from decision.md; decided in advance)

> 1. எடை உங்க கண் முன்னாடிதான் போடுவோம். டிஸ்ப்ளேயை நீங்களே பாருங்க.
> 2. முதல்ல 5 கிலோ டெஸ்ட் எடையை தொங்கவிட்டு, தராசு சரியா காட்டுதான்னு உங்க முன்னாடியே காட்டுவோம்.
> 3. சந்தேகம்னா ஒரு சாக்கை உங்க கடை தராசுலயும் போட்டு compare பண்ணலாம்.
> 4. ரசீதுல கிலோ × ரேட் = ₹ எழுதி ஒரு காப்பி உங்ககிட்ட கொடுப்போம். WhatsApp-லயும் அனுப்புவோம்.
>
> திருப்தி இல்லைன்னா அந்த வாரம் கொடுக்க வேண்டாம்.
>
> *A fair question. 1. We weigh in front of you; read the display yourself. 2. First we hang a 5 kg test weight so you can see the scale reads right. 3. If in doubt, weigh a sack on your own shop scale and compare. 4. The receipt shows kg × rate = ₹; you keep a copy, and we send one on WhatsApp too. If you're not satisfied, don't sell that week.*

### 4.9 When an eatery says "someone already buys my shells"
> அப்படியா, நல்லதுங்க! ஒரு நிமிஷம். அவங்க கிலோவுக்கு எவ்வளவு தர்றாங்க? எத்தனை நாளுக்கு ஒரு தடவை வர்றாங்க? உங்க முன்னாடி எடை போடுறாங்களா? ரசீது தர்றாங்களா?
>
> *Oh, that's good! Just a minute: how much do they pay per kg? How often do they come? Do they weigh in front of you? Do they give a receipt?*

**Record the answers.** They feed the kill criteria. Then reply according to what you heard:

- **They pay more than this week's rate:**
  > அப்போ அவங்ககிட்டயே கொடுங்க, அதுதான் உங்களுக்கு நல்லது. எங்க நம்பரை மட்டும் save பண்ணிக்கோங்க. வாரா வாரம் ரேட் அனுப்புறோம், compare பண்ணிக்கலாம்.
  >
  > *Then keep selling to them; that's best for you. Just save our number. We send the rate every week, so you can compare.*
- **They pay less, come irregularly, or give no receipt:**
  > ஒரே ஒரு வாரம் எங்களுக்கு கொடுத்து பாருங்க. ரசீதுல கிலோ × ரேட் = ₹ இருக்கும். அவங்க கொடுத்ததோட compare பண்ணி, எது நல்லதோ அதை செய்யுங்க. நாங்க யாரையும் குறை சொல்ல வரல.
  >
  > *Try us for just one week. The receipt shows kg × rate = ₹. Compare it with what they pay and do whatever's better for you. We're not here to criticise anyone.*
- **It's a relative, a staff member, or someone who gave an advance:**
  > சரிங்க, அதை மாத்த சொல்லல. தேவைப்பட்டா கூப்பிடுங்க.
  >
  > *Understood. We're not asking you to change that. Call us if you ever need us.*

**Never** criticise the other buyer, promise to "beat any price", or quote a rate the engine hasn't produced.

## 5. Trust mechanics

| # | Mechanic | How to do it | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **Weigh in front of the owner** | Bring the sack to the counter, not the back of the auto. Zero the scale in front of the owner, hang the sack, **read the weight aloud** and turn the display towards the owner. Deduct a standard **sack tare**: weigh one empty sack in front of each new owner once and write it on the receipt | Time only |
| 5.2 | **Digital hanging scale** | Test it at the start of every route and in front of any owner who asks, using a **5 kg test weight** (§4.3). A stamped scale is also a strong trust line | Scale ₹1.5–3k (MVP budget). Test weight in §8 |
| 5.3 | **Receipt on the spot** | Numbered duplicate paper receipt (§4.5), with the owner's copy handed over before we leave. A WhatsApp copy follows. **Paper is primary; WhatsApp is optional** (decision.md: it works on cash and paper) | Receipt books in §8 |
| 5.4 | **Fixed pickup day** | Same weekday and same time window every week. Reminder the evening before. If we must change the day (Deepavali, heavy rain), say so **at least 2 days ahead** [A]. **A missed pickup costs more trust than a low rate** | Discipline |
| 5.5 | **Published weekly rate** | Every Monday, by Status and broadcast (§4.4), with the dates it is valid and **why it changed**. **One rate for every shop on the route**, with no private deals. Explain the grades **at sign-up, before the first pickup**, never as a surprise. Grade in front of the owner. **If the owner disagrees, don't argue:** leave that sack to dry and take it next week at full weight | Tip cards in §8 |

## 6. 90-day calendar (Thu 1 Oct to Tue 29 Dec 2026)

**Key dates**
- **Ayudha Pooja:** Mon 19 Oct in Tamil Nadu (some calendars say 20 Oct). Vijayadasami is Tue 20 Oct.
- **NE monsoon:** the IMD's normal onset over coastal TN is **20 Oct ± 7 days**. In 2023–25 it arrived on 15–16 Oct.
- **Deepavali:** Sat 7 and Sun 8 Nov.
- **Karthigai Deepam:** Tue 24 Nov.
- **Tamil months:** Aippasi starts about 18 Oct, Karthigai about 17 Nov and Margazhi about 16 Dec **[check the local panchangam]**.

## 7. Metrics and targets

The Day-14 column comes from decision.md. Days 30, 60 and 90 are **my targets [A]**, built on the red team's route assumption of 40 eateries × about 40 kg/week ≈ 1,600 kg per route-day.

| Metric | How it's measured | Day 14 (validation) | Day 30 (31 Oct) | Day 60 (30 Nov) | Day 90 (29 Dec) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Eateries signed** | Took sacks, gave a phone number and agreed a pickup day | **≥ 25** (stop if < 10) | 40 (1 route) | 80 (2 routes) | 120 (3 routes) |
| **Active eateries** | Gave shells at least once in the last 2 pickups ÷ signed | Report it | ≥ 80% | ≥ 80% | ≥ 80% |
| **kg per week** | Net kg on receipts | **≥ 1 t over 2 weeks** | 1.2–1.6 t | ~3 t | 4–5 t |
| **kg per eatery per week** | kg ÷ active eateries | Compare with the 40 kg assumption | ≥ 30 kg | ≥ 35 kg | ≥ 35 kg |
| **Net ₹/kg** | (buyer ₹ − eatery ₹ − auto − freight − staff bonuses) ÷ kg bought | **≥ ₹3** (stop if < ₹1.5) | ≥ ₹3 | ≥ ₹3 | ≥ ₹3 |
| **Repeat pickups** | Eateries that gave shells this week and last week ÷ those that gave last week | – | ≥ 80% | ≥ 85% | ≥ 85% |
| **Moisture and kernel loss** | (kg bought − kg paid for by the buyer) ÷ kg bought | Measure it | ≤ 10% | ≤ 10% (monsoon) | ≤ 10% |
| **Buyers committed weekly, in writing** | WhatsApp "OK" | **≥ 1** | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| **Unresolved scale disputes** | Log every dispute | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **Marketing ₹ per signed eatery** | Spend ÷ signed | Report it | ≤ ₹200 | ≤ ₹150 | ≤ ₹125 |

**What these numbers mean in money [A, red team §3]:** about **₹6,000 net per route-day** at the base case, so **₹12–26k a month per weekly route** before management costs.

**Stop rules, rolling after validation (my proposal, adapted from decision.md):**
- **Net spread under ₹1.5/kg on 2 lots in a row:** pause pickups and re-quote the buyers.
- **Buyer's delivered price below about ₹25/kg while eateries still expect ₹18 or more:** pause, reset expectations with the weekly broadcast, and resume only when the rate engine gives ≥ ₹3/kg net.
- **Most eateries on a route start getting ≥ ₹20/kg from others:** don't chase them. Stop that route.
- **Active eateries fall below 50% for 2 weeks:** fix the pickup day or the rate message before adding anything.

## 9. Marketing risks and how to handle them

| Risk | What it looks like | Prevention | If it happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Promising a fixed rate** | A collector says "always ₹20"; a rate is printed on a flyer or banner; a reel shows a number; the buyer price then falls | Rates only via the Monday broadcast, with valid-from and valid-to dates. **No numbers on anything durable.** The flyer box is filled in by hand. Train collectors on the §4.10 lines | Honour this week's announced rate for this week's pickups. Next Monday, broadcast the new rate **with the reason**. Never quietly pay less than the rate on the receipt |
| **Scale disputes** | "Your scale is short"; staff claim weight went missing | §5.1–5.2: test weight every route and every time someone asks, cross-check on the owner's own scale, read the weight aloud, give a receipt. Check whether stamping is needed [check] | Re-weigh on the owner's scale. If the gap is beyond a small tolerance you set in advance [A], pay on the owner's reading for that pickup, log it, and re-test or replace our scale. Cheap trust |
| **Monsoon moisture** | Wet, heavy sacks; moisture loss above 10%; arguments over grade 2 | Reel 3, tip cards, broadcast tips from mid-October. Explain the grades at sign-up. Set the deduction % from the buyer's own deduction. Cover the goods auto [A] | Don't argue: leave wet sacks to dry until next week. If loss stays above 10%, raise the grade-2 deduction openly on Monday, with the reason |
| **Informal competitors** | A trader starts paying ₹1–2 more on our streets, or visits right after we publish the rate | Compete on the **fixed day, receipt and honest scale**, not price (Kerala traders run on about ₹2/kg). Share the rate only with signed eateries, not on public boards | Use §4.9. Never match a price the engine can't pay. If most of a route moves to ≥ ₹20/kg, stop that route (§7) |
| **Staff perks** | Sacks come in half-empty; shells "go missing"; staff are hostile | §2.2 options A and B; §4.7 pitch; survey Q4 flag | Talk to the owner and staff together. Put the staff share on the receipt |
| **Expectations set by the news** | "The paper says ₹35 a kilo" | Never quote factory or Kerala prices in customer copy | Use the §4.10 script and the §4.0 logic |
| **Wrong suppliers** | A temple committee or tender-coconut vendors ask us to collect | §2.4 exclusion | Politely decline with the §2.4 lines |
| **Wrong buyers** | A buyer can't show who they are, or wants credit | §2.3 due diligence; cash or UPI on delivery only | Don't sell. Move to the next quote |
| **Fake social proof** | Temptation to post a scripted testimonial or a staged weigh-in | Real footage, written consent, owners in their own words only | Take down anything that isn't real |
